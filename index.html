Three steps from data to a lean

A side becomes a lean when our probability from the game data sits above what a book’s price implies by more than the model’s own margin of error. Other books’ prices never set the grade.

In a replay of past seasons these leans did not beat the closing price. They become picks when the live record beats the close.

Game data

Build the game model

Each team’s results and margins this season, recent form, rest and back-to-backs, the injury report weighted by scoring, and home edge become a projected margin and total for every game, and from those our probability for every side.

Prices

Shop the price

Moneyline, spread and total prices from the major U.S. sportsbooks are pulled in every day before the slate. They are the number our probability has to clear and where to bet it; the market’s own probability is shown beside ours for reference only.

Edge = our % − implied %

Find the edge

When a book’s price implies a lower probability than ours, that gap is the edge. It must clear the side’s own bar: the model’s margin of error there, from game data alone, never from other books’ prices. The bar rises when the model knows less, early in a season or for a team with few games, and falls to 3 points as the season fills in. Two points past it is Medium, five High; a thin sample stays Low. At most one lean a game.

Questions people ask

How is the lean of the day chosen?

Everything on today’s games goes into one pool: sides, totals and moneylines from the game model, and player props. The one with the highest chance to hit that still has an edge at its price is named. The parlay is the likeliest two from different games at one book, each with an edge there. A prop’s chance is the props board’s number, checked against how graded props have finished. Both lock with the 3:30 PM ET run, earlier if the game starts earlier, and are never swapped. A day with no edge on any of them names none.

What does “edge” mean?

Edge is the difference between our probability for the side, from the game data (this season’s results and margins, form, rest, injuries, home edge), and the probability implied by the best available price. A +3% edge means we have the bet winning 3 percentage points more often than that price suggests, which is roughly +6% expected value at even odds if our probability is right. It is the model’s read against the price, not proof that the price is wrong. Nothing is graded against other books’ prices; the market’s own number appears on each breakdown for reference only.

Why does one lean need 3 points of edge and another 10?

Each side’s bar is the model’s own margin of error on it. A side the model knows little about needs a bigger edge before it is posted, so no sport floods the board because its numbers are noisy, and every sport is ranked the same way: points past its own bar.

What happens if the price moves after a lean is posted?

Nothing is taken back. It stays on the card and is graded at the price it was posted at. If a later check finds the edge under its bar, the card says “Below its bar now”; if the edge is gone, “Edge gone”.

Does the model guarantee winners?

No. Nothing does. In a replay of past seasons the model’s selections lost money at the closing price in the NHL, the NBA and the NFL: it knows less than the market, so most of its edges land on underdogs. That is why they are called leans. Read them as the model’s view of each game, ranked by how likely it makes each side, and judge them by the record on the performance page.

How are results tracked?

Every lean is logged with its odds at the time of publication and graded to one unit of risk. Results are shown on the performance page and never edited after the fact.

Which sportsbooks do you use for odds?

Prices come from The Odds API (DraftKings, FanDuel, BetMGM, Caesars, BetRivers, Fanatics, ESPN Bet, Bovada, BetOnline), with SportsGameOdds adding more books on each side’s line. They are where to bet it, nothing more: the grade comes from the game data, not from any book. Each lean names the book offering the best price, and its result is scored at that price. The market’s de-vigged number is shown for reference only.

Is this legal where I live?

EdgeLine AI only provides analysis. Whether you can place a bet depends on the laws where you are. Check your local regulations and only bet with licensed operators.
