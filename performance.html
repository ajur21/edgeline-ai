How grading works. Every pick is now made on game data: our probability for the side comes from each team’s results and margins this season, recent form, rest, the injury report and home edge, and a pick is a side where that probability beats the best price by 3 points or more. Nothing is graded against other books’ prices. Picks logged before the game model went live were made on the price edge against the sharpest book’s line; they keep their own record under Record by grade and are marked “price grade” in the results table. Each pick is graded to one unit of risk at the odds published. A win at -110 returns +0.91u; a loss is -1.00u; a push is 0. Return on investment is units won divided by units risked. Closing line value (CLV) is a market measure: it compares the price we published with the market’s last pre-game line, the closing probability minus the probability implied by our price, in points. It says whether the price was good, not whether the read of the game was right, and it takes no part in the grade. Positive CLV means the market moved our way after we posted, which is the best available evidence that an edge was real rather than lucky. The close is DraftKings' closing number as published by ESPN (free, available after every game); when a spread or total closes on a different number than ours, the column shows whether the line moved toward (▲) or against (▼) the pick instead of a price figure. Between the two daily odds runs, a lighter job refreshes DraftKings' open and current numbers every two hours so each breakdown shows the market’s drift.
