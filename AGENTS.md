# EdgeLine AI — agent brief

Read this first. It is the hand-off document for any agent or developer working on this site.

## What this is

A static marketing and product website for an AI sports betting picks service. Nine pages, no framework, no build step. Open `index.html` in a browser or serve the folder from any static host.

"EdgeLine AI" is a placeholder brand. All copy, prices, picks and performance numbers are placeholders and are labelled "Sample data" in the UI. Do not remove those labels until real data is wired in.

## Conventions (keep these)

- **Ordering rule (owner decision, 2026-11-04, refined twice the same day): game day first, then positive edge, then likeliest.** The owner's words: "order things by date as well. no reason for a play from tomorrow to pop up before a play from today." Every list of picks or sides (the home page's ranked list and ticker, picks grid and table, market board, best parlays, the digest's three likeliest and slate, the never-empty fallbacks) is sorted first by the Eastern calendar day of the game (`EdgeLine.dayKey(p)`: `commence` through `Intl.DateTimeFormat` in America/New_York, falling back to the `start` label the way the digest parses it, where "Today"/"Tonight" is the data's day and a bare weekday is always a later day), then in two tiers: sides with a positive edge, then everything else (a locked pick that has slipped under its bar sits between the two: see Locked picks under The per-side bar on the page); within each tier by our probability (`modelProb`, from the game model) descending, with edge as the tiebreaker (`EdgeLine.sortByProb` / `worthFirst`, which compares `dayCmp` first; `marketRows` does the same for market rows, keeping the likelier side of each market; `bestParlays` orders tickets by the day of their earliest leg, then the ticket’s probability (its legs’ probabilities multiplied), then EV, and each ticket carries `day`). The day boundary is visible: a day heading (`.day-divider` in the picks grid and the digest slate via `EdgeLine.dayDivider(key)`, `tr.day-divider` from `marketTable({ dayDivider: true })`; text like "Tomorrow · Thu Nov 6" or "Saturday · Nov 8", built from `EdgeLine.dayLabel(key)`, which is relative to the current Eastern date) opens each day whenever the list spans more than one, on the default order and on the start-time order. Rank chips still number the whole list in display order, so #1 is today's likeliest. Real picks always have a positive edge, so the tiers only show on the fallbacks and the market board, where a dashed divider ("Priced against you from here") marks the boundary within each day that has both tiers (`marketTable({ divider })`, `.grid-divider` in the picks grid fallback). The order must be visible, not implied: a rank chip on every card and row (`EdgeLine.rankChip`, "#1 · Likeliest" on top), a probability bar on every card (fill = our probability, tick = what the price implies, hairline = 50%), “To hit” (our probability) as the lead metric, and a one-line "how to read" under each ranked heading. Sort controls on the picks page and the market board default to "Likeliest first"; the other orders are opt-in. ## Data pipeline

`scripts/update-data.js` is the "backend". `.github/workflows/track-prices.yml` runs `node scripts/update-data.js --track` **hourly for sixteen hours**, 10 AM to 1 AM Eastern (15:00-06:00 UTC): no Odds API requests, it refreshes DraftKings' open and current numbers from ESPN for every board pick and, for games starting within `sgoTrackWindowHours` (8), every book's price from SportsGameOdds, re-reads who plays for every posted NBA pick (the official injury report and ESPN's NBA list, two requests; see NBA availability under step 3), rebuilds the breakdowns, **grades every game that has finished** (free scores only, see step 5; it then rewrites `data/performance.js`, and leaves it alone when nothing was graded and no close was repaired), rewrites `data/picks.js` (keeping `generatedAt`, adding `trackedAt`) and commits when the data, the log or the feeds changed (`git status --porcelain` over `data/picks.js`, `data/picks-log.json`, `data/performance.js`, `data/props.js` (the surplus's props refresh and, since 2026-11-10, graded props), `data/props-log.json` (the props record's state), `data/budget.json` (the spenders' state), `data/nfl-week.js` and `data/nfl-week-log.json` (the NFL week, since 2026-11-10), `picks.ics`, `feed.xml`; `trackedAt` alone changes every run, so in practice it commits hourly). Results therefore land within the hour of a final, not at the next full run (owner complaint, 2026-11-05: picks "pop and go"). Cost: ESPN is free and unmetered; SportsGameOdds spends one entity per board game per hourly run inside the 8-hour window (roughly 6 games × 8 runs = 48 a day on a busy board, about 1,450 a month, plus the full runs), with `sgoReserve` as the backstop; since 2026-11-08 the track run also spends the cycle's spare entities (`sgoSurplus`: the window widened to 12 hours, props lines for NBA and NFL games starting within three hours, the college board; see the request budget) and its log line says what it spent. Since 2026-11-10 it also locks and grades props (`update-props.js track`: ESPN box scores, free, no entity and no credit; see Props research and lines), and its log line ends with the props record. The same day it gained a second step, `node scripts/nfl-week.js --track`, which keeps the NFL week's states, scores and straight-up grades current (one ESPN request while a game of the week is in play, none otherwise; it never touches a projection and can never fail the job; see NFL week hub (2026-11-10)). The full odds run cannot go hourly on The Odds API's free tier (3 credits per sport per run; two runs a day is the ceiling, and only for some sports: see the request budget). `.github/workflows/update-data.yml` runs the full pipeline twice a day, 14:00 UTC (9 AM Eastern in winter, 10 AM in summer; it was 17:00 UTC until 2026-11-05, when the owner moved it earlier because the replay audit found the model's information is worth more against prices nearer the open) and 20:30 UTC (late afternoon, before the evening slates), and on demand, then commits `data/` and redeploys. Since 2026-11-10 each full run also projects the NFL week right after the odds run (`node scripts/nfl-week.js`: ESPN and the National Weather Service only, no credit and no entity, in a step that can never fail the job; see NFL week hub (2026-11-10)). It passes `--slot morning|afternoon|manual` (a scheduled run from its cron line, a dispatch from its `slot` input), which decides which sports the run fetches (step 1). **Who starts them (2026-11-05):** GitHub's schedule fires hours late on this repo (on 2026-11-04 the 17:00 run started at 22:15 UTC and the 20:30 one at 00:59), so two Claude routines (claude.ai/code/routines, "EdgeLine morning data run" and "EdgeLine afternoon data run") dispatch the workflow on time with `slot=morning` / `slot=afternoon` (`gh workflow run update-data.yml -f slot=…`). The schedule stays as the fallback: the workflow's `gate` job (`scripts/slot-due.js`, tested by `scripts/slot-test.js`) skips any run for a slot that a full run has already covered since that slot's time (picks.js `generatedAt`), so a late duplicate never spends the credits twice; a manual dispatch (`slot` manual, the default) always runs. The morning slot passes `--lines nfl` to the props step whichever way it was started (NFL lines only: the NBA's come on the afternoon run; see Props research and lines). A 4 AM run found no edges; the two-run schedule is an experiment, and each logged pick records `loggedAt` so the log shows which run produces edges. Drop the weaker one later. It needs the `ODDS_API_KEY` repository secret (a key from https://the-odds-api.com; the free tier is 500 requests/month).

How a pick is made (all in `update-data.js`, tunable in `RULES`):
1. **Which sports a run fetches (2026-11-05; the owner: "we still need to do nfl and mlb and mls").** Sports: NFL, NBA, MLB, NHL, the Premier League, MLS and, since 2026-11-08, college football (NCAAF, the FBS: `SPORTS`; La Liga is switched off to stay inside the free tier). For each, fetch moneyline, spread and total prices (soccer: the three-way moneyline and the total) for ten named bookmakers: Pinnacle plus nine bettable U.S. books (ten bookmakers cost the same as one region: 3 credits per sport, 2 for soccer), only when there is a game to price, cheapest check first: (a) The Odds API's `/sports` list (free) leaves out a sport out of season; (b) the **slot rule** (`slots`, `morningDays` and `runDays` in `SPORTS`; `slotAllows`, `runDayAllows`), sport by sport: the **NBA** on the afternoon run only (since 2026-11-08: its afternoon read of the official injury report beat the morning one and the morning price had already moved most of the way to the close; an NBA game whose clubs are not both on the report yet is held, `RULES.availabilityHold`: see NBA availability under step 3 and The model against the market); the **NHL** (and MLB, in season) on both runs; the **NFL** on the afternoon run (Saturday's covers Sunday, Wednesday's Thursday night) plus, since 2026-11-08, the morning run on its `morningDays` (Sunday, Thursday, Saturday) when one of its games kicks off before that day's afternoon run (ESPN's `early` count; ESPN unreadable: Sundays only): every Sunday's 1 PM and London slates and Thanksgiving's early game, never TNF, SNF or MNF (see NFL model (2026-11-08)); the **Premier League and MLS** on the afternoon run only (the 36-hour window still reaches the next day: Friday's covers Saturday's soccer); **college football** on Friday's afternoon run only (`runDays: [5]`, since 2026-11-08): its 36-hour window holds Friday night and all of Saturday, 55 to 65 of a November week's 60 to 70 FBS games; the MAC's Tuesday and Wednesday nights, Thursday games and the weekday bowls are priced only by the surplus spender (f), when the month has credits to spare (see College football (2026-11-08)); a manual run fetches every sport, any day; (c) **ESPN's scoreboard** (`espnGames`, free, read through the context source so the model's later read is cached) for every Eastern day the 36-hour window touches: a sport with no game starting inside the window is not asked at all, which covers NFL Thursdays, Saturdays and holidays and stops off-season spending; only when a day cannot be read and no game was found does the sport's old `days` rule decide (NFL Wed/Thu/Sat/Sun/Mon, Premier League Fri-Sun, MLS Tue/Wed/Fri-Sun, college football Fri; NBA/NHL/MLB every day); (d) the **morning guard** (`fetchDecision`): a morning run spends only what the month can spare after `RULES.afternoonReserve` (10) credits for every afternoon run left before the credits reset on the 1st (The Odds API's FAQ), plus the floor (`RULES.minRequestsLeft`, 10 since 2026-11-08, 25 before), so when the month runs short the morning runs give way first; (f) the **surplus spender** (`oddsSurplus`, 2026-11-08): after the protected fetches, optional ones with whatever the protected schedule will not need before the reset (see the request budget); (e) the odds request itself asks only for games starting inside the window (`commenceTimeFrom` / `commenceTimeTo`, `oddsParams`), and per The Odds API's docs a request that returns no events costs nothing (should the API refuse the window, the request is repeated without it). Every full run prints one line for the Actions log (`planLine`): what it fetched, what each fetch cost (the `x-requests-last` header), what it skipped and why, and the credits left and used.
2. Per event and market, group books by exact line and keep the largest group (at least 3 books). The books' own number (`marketFair`, a reference that takes no part in the grade since 2026-11-04) is Pinnacle's de-vigged price for that line (the sharp reference). If Pinnacle posts a neighbouring line instead (NFL and NBA spreads and totals only, at most `maxAnchorShift` = 1 point away), its de-vigged price is **carried** to the books' line at a per-sport slope (`LINE_VALUE`: NFL 2.2%/pt spreads, 1.5%/pt totals; NBA 2.8%/pt and 1.4%/pt), the two sides renormalised to one; the pick records `anchorShift` and an `anchorNote`, the rationale and breakdown say so, and the breakdown files it under notes rather than support ("a fair estimate, not a quote"). Hockey, baseball and soccer lines move in steps too big to convert. Only when neither applies does the de-vigged average of the other books stand in. Until 2026-11-04 this number was the grade and the site called it "Fair"; it now lives in `marketFair` / `marketEdge` and is shown as "where the market sits".
3. **The game model grades every side (owner rule, 2026-11-04: plays and props are graded on game data, never against other books' prices).** Before pricing, `model.forEvent()` finds the game on ESPN's scoreboard for its Eastern date, reads both teams' schedules (completed regular-season games before tip-off: points for and against, margins, the last five; soccer marks league matches with the season's own id rather than type 2, which until 2026-11-04 made the filter drop every Premier League game, so no EPL game had a model), the league injury report and rest (`restFromSchedule`: calendar days in Eastern time, so a game the night before is a back-to-back whatever the tip-off hours say). `gameModel()` turns that into an expected margin (home minus away) and total: each team's rating is (1 − form) × season margin + form × last-five margin (form 0.1 in the NBA, NFL, NHL, EPL and MLS, 0 in MLB), shrunk toward zero by gp / (gp + k) (k: NBA 6, NFL 6, EPL 8, MLS 20, NHL 35, MLB 60). **Carry-over:** where the club has last season in the league (`lastSeasonParam` names the season as ESPN numbers it: NBA and NHL by the year a season ends, NFL and EPL by the year it starts, MLB and MLS by their year; `scheduleSeason` fetches it, one free request a club a run), the rating starts from part of last season’s margin per game instead of zero: (gp × raw + carry.k × carry.c × last) / (gp + carry.k), with carry NBA 60% over 10 games, NFL 40% over 6, NHL 25% over 40, MLB 50% over 80, EPL 75% over 15, MLS 25% over 20 (fitted 2026-11-05 on held-out seasons, results only; early-season error fell about 5% in the NBA and EPL, 3% in the NFL); a promoted club keeps the old k and starts from zero; plus the sport's home edge (NBA 1.9, NFL 2.3, NHL 0.24, MLB 0.03, EPL 0.24, MLS 0.5), a back-to-back penalty and a 3+ days' rest bonus (NBA −1.5 / +0.5, NHL −0.25 / +0.05, EPL rest only), and injuries in the sport's points (an NBA player listed Out costs 12% of his points a game from `data/faces.js`, 1.2 when unknown; an NFL quarterback 4; a goalie 0.5; a starting pitcher 0.3; day-to-day or questionable counts half, and an NBA listing from the league's official report counts at how often its status sits (see NBA availability below); a goalie or a quarterback counts once per team, the costliest listing, because only one plays: `SPORT[].single`, fixed 2026-11-04 after two Rangers goalies listed Out stacked to a full goal); the total is the four scoring averages halved, each first pulled toward the league's scoring per team-game this season by `kTotal` games (NFL 5, MLS 15, MLB 20, EPL 30, NHL 80; the NBA keeps raw averages), read once a run from ESPN's standings (`leagueGoalsFrom`: points for over games played, or over wins + losses + ties where the table has no games column), `leagueGoals` as the fallback. An MLB pitcher appears by turn (a healthy starter in one game in five), so his count is the stretch of the team's games from his first appearance to his last. The log is asked for without a season, so ESPN returns the player's latest season and names it: an older one means no games this season (`appearances` in `context.js`; with `?season=` the NHL answers a player with no games with a 404). see NFL model (2026-11-08)); what this step says of the NFL still holds for its totals, its other injury listings and the fallback when nflverse cannot be read. `sideProb()` reads a league's own distribution of final margins and totals, tilted to the game's projected margin and total, wherever the calibration chose it: the NHL (`NHL_SHAPE`, both markets: no ties, overtime's extra goal, a two-goal lead becoming three with the goalie pulled), MLB (`MLB_SHAPE`, both: one-run finals are 28%, and the margins are kept from the home side's view because the home team does not bat in the ninth when ahead, so home teams win 53% while out-scoring visitors by 0.03 runs), the EPL (`EPL_SHAPE`, both, with the draw: a quarter of matches), MLS (`MLS_SHAPE`, both, with the draw; see The model against the market below): p' = 1 / (1 + e^−(a + b·logit p)) on the home or over side, the other side its complement, fitted on held-out seasons from results only (each side's probability read at the line the game was played at; no price takes part). NBA spreads and totals (b 0.05 and 0.04) and NFL spreads and totals (0.08 and 0) read about even, because at the books' lines our numbers did worse than a coin flip on every held-out season; the NBA moneyline is stretched (a −0.03, b 1.17: it was too flat); the NHL, MLB, EPL and MLS, and the NFL moneyline, are left raw (no map beat the raw numbers held out; MLB and MLS untested). `rawSideProb` is the model alone; `sideProb` (what every caller reads, `noise()` included, so a calibrated side's bar is in calibrated points) applies the map; a side the map pulls toward even says so in its reasons ("Past seasons: our totals at the books' lines did no better than a coin flip, so this side reads 50.8% (the model alone 69.6%)"). A market with no signal (`model.hasSignal`: a calibration slope under 0.5, so NBA and NFL spreads and totals) is never a pick or a watch, whatever the price: its sides read about even, and a plus-money price would otherwise put a coin flip past the 3-point floor; it stays on the market board as information. `priceWithModel()` writes that probability into `fair` / `modelProb` for every priced side and the edge as our probability − implied(best bettable price, never Pinnacle); a game with no ESPN record (no scoreboard match, no completed games) has no probability on any side and can never be a pick (`modelled` false on its market rows). **The bar (Phase 2, 2026-11-05; the owner said the board "feels favored to sports" and approved "fewer, steadier picks"): every side has its own.** `model.noise()` is the model's own error on that side from the size of the sample alone: the standard error of the rating difference behind the margin, sd × √(1/(gpHome + kHome) + 1/(gpAway + kAway)) with k the prior's weight in games (`carry.k` where the club has last season, else the shrinkage `k`), or for a total sdTotal / 2 × √(1/(gpHome + kTotal) + 1/(gpAway + kTotal)), moved through the side's own probability at its line one error each way (the mean move, so a draw still gets one). The bar is `RULES.barZ` (1) of those errors and never under `RULES.minEdge` (3 points, the floor): early in a season about 9 to 12 points on an NBA side, 6 to 7 on an NHL one, 7 to 15 in the NFL, 3 on most NHL totals, falling toward 3 as the games pile up. Checked against held-out seasons (results only): the formula's early-season margin error, NBA 4.9 / NHL 0.50 / MLB 0.68 / EPL 0.51, against a measured 5.4 / 0.52 / 0.63 / 0.60 (NFL 5.2 against 3.3, cautious; MLS 0.45 against 0.51, 2025). A flat 3-point bar sat inside that noise, so the noisiest sport filled the board: on the 11-04 board it took 9 picks on 8 games, all High; the per-side bar took 8 (NHL 4, NBA 3, NFL 1), mostly Medium. The tier is how far the edge clears the side's own bar (`past` = edge − bar): Lean past it, Medium 2 points past (`RULES.medium`), High 5 (`RULES.high`); a thin sample (under four games behind either team, `model.thin`) is capped at Lean. A pick logged before 2026-11-05 has no `bar` and was graded on the flat 3 / 5 / 8. Each pick records `bar`, `past` and `barParts` { error, units, unit, gp, floor }; the rationale, factors and breakdown give the bar in words ("Its bar is 9.2 points: the model's own margin of error on this side, with 8 games behind the thinner team (±4.8 points on the margin). It clears it by 3.1."). Each pick records `basis: "game"`, `model` (margin, total, sd, gp, thin, `parts`, and `reasons` for and against the side), `marketFair` / `marketEdge`, `ev` (expected profit per unit) and a rationale that names the book, states the rule and gives the market number as a reference. Only one side of a market can be picked. The price-edge grade is gone from the pipeline; picks logged before the change have no `basis` and read as "price" in the record. NCAAF (FBS) runs end to end: the fetch (Friday's afternoon run, see step 1 and the budget), a game model from game data only (`scripts/ncaaf.js`), the per-side bar, one pick a game, the five-a-sport day cap, grading from ESPN's finals and performance by sport. It is `sport: "NCAAF"`, league "NCAAF", shown as "College football". **The held-out replay backs no college market, so it posts no leans:** every college side is on the market board and the Games page with our probability; none is a lean or a watch.

- **The model** (`scripts/ncaaf.js`, self-contained; `model.forEvent` hands college games to `ncaaf.forEvent`). 138 FBS teams play 12 games inside conferences that meet a few times a season, so a raw average margin mostly measures the schedule. Every team gets a schedule-adjusted rating from every FBS game this season: a ridge fit of home margin = rating gap + home edge 2.8 (none at a neutral site), each margin counted up to 35 points, each rating pulled toward a prior worth 3 games, the prior 90% of last season's final rating (fitted with almost no shrinkage, centred on the FBS mean). Totals: an offence and a defence rating per team the same way (6 games of prior at 40% of last season), around the FBS mean. FCS opponents are one pooled group (its mean fitted, 2 games of prior each), only so FBS results against them count at their size. Probabilities: the normal curve at 16 points on the margin and 16 on the total; the bar is `model.noise` on it (16 × √(1/(gp+3) + 1/(gp+3)): about 6.5 points of margin at 9 games each, so about 16 points on a close moneyline, more early in a season and less on a lopsided game, and the 3-point floor on spreads and totals). No injury cost (ESPN's college report is thin and uneven across 138 teams and cannot be replayed) and no rest term (a bye's +2 points fitted on 2022-2023 by 0.015 points of error, then did worse held out: 16.573 against 16.556). Data: ESPN's public scoreboard, one request a week with `groups=80` (every game with an FBS team), this season to date and all of last season, about 33 requests and 28 MB on a run that fetches NCAAF (`src.week` in `context.js`); the date scoreboard asks `&groups=80&limit=300` (`SB_QUERY`). The pipeline's requests use Node's own client name, never a browser's.
- **Fitted on 2022-2023, results only** (2021 and 2022 as the priors; coordinate descent on margin and total error): margin error 16.71 points against 20.07 for the home edge alone; held out on 2024 to 2026 it was 16.56. On the 2,121 held-out games with closing lines: 16.49 against the closing spread's 15.03, totals 16.87 against the closing total's 15.83.
- **The replay, the audit's method** (2,154 FBS-against-FBS games held out: 2024, 2025 and 2026 to Nov 7, each replayed with only the games before its date; ESPN's core odds, ESPN BET 2024-25 and DraftKings 2026, open and close, for evaluation only). Moneyline (2,033 games): log-loss 0.5777 against the closing market's 0.5423 (coin flip 0.6931), calibrated against results (slope 1.00 ± 0.06) but flatter than the market (the closing favourite's side: us 67.0%, the market 71.4%, won 72.2%); with both in one regression the model's weight is 0.00 ± 0.10 beyond the close and 0.07 ± 0.11 beyond the open. Spreads at the books' lines (2,111): 0.7495, worse than a coin flip (slope −0.03 ± 0.06; at the open 0.7427); totals (2,126): 0.7313 (slope −0.02 ± 0.07; open 0.7265). The result moved −0.02 ± 0.05 points per point of our margin's gap from the closing spread (−0.01 ± 0.05 from the open) and −0.10 ± 0.06 per point of our total's gap (−0.04 ± 0.07 from the open). Today's rule on the raw model, at the close: 341 picks (0.16 a game), 70% underdogs, ROI −8.5% ± 5.3 (moneyline 33 at −47.1% ± 19.0, 91% underdogs; spreads 70 at −4.1% ± 11.6; totals 238 at −4.4% ± 6.2); at the open 315, −0.4% ± 6.2 (moneyline 38 at +29.4% ± 28.9; spreads 63 at −2.5% ± 12.2; totals 214 at −5.1% ± 6.6).

## The market board and the never-empty rule

Every full run writes `data/market.js`: both sides of every main-line market it priced (`priceOutcomes` rows with `qualifies`), with event, start label, best price and book, every book's price, our probability (`modelProb`, null when the game has no model, with `modelled` false), edge (ours, null without a model), EV, `marketFair` / `marketEdge` (the books' number, reference only), `model` (margin, total, gp, thin), anchor and `anchorShift`, each with its own `bar` and `past` (null without a model), flagged `status`: `pick` (a pending pick on the board, with `pickId`), `watch` (edge of at least half its own bar, `RULES.watchShare`, and at least `RULES.watchEdge` = a point and a half; never a pick) or `none`. The file carries `basis: "game"`, `threshold` (the floor of every bar, 3 points), `watch`, `barRule` { floor, z, watchShare, medium, high }, `bars` (per sport: min, median and max bar across its modelled sides) and `modelled` (games with a model). Sorted by our edge, unmodelled sides last; `--track` runs leave it alone. A sport a full run does not fetch (its slot, the morning guard, the quota) keeps its rows from the last file for games still ahead (`carriedRows`), each with `pricedAt` (when its prices were read) and its pick / watch flag re-read from the log, so Sunday's morning run never empties the NFL from the market board and the Games page an hour before kickoff. The picks page shows it as the **Market board** (`#market`): summary line, sport / market / status / search filters, 40 rows at a time with a pick row linking to its card.

**Owner rule (2026-11-04): nothing on the site stays empty.** When no pick clears the bar, every slot falls back to the closest markets, always labelled Watch / No bet and never dressed as a play: the home ticker and the home page's ranked list (since 2026-11-11: with no play that has an edge, the open leans that slipped under their bar, then the watchlist, then the closest markets, "Worth a look tonight"), the play-of-the-day slot (`potdCard(null)` shows the nearest miss with a note, when nothing is locked, there is no front-runner and the day is not closed), the parlay slot and the best-parlays list (`pulseCard`: games, markets, watch count, top three), the picks grid (market cards: status badge instead of a confidence badge, no parlay button, a Market board link instead of Full breakdown), and the digest (lede, tiles, the three likeliest, slate retitled "The watch list"). `EdgeLine.marketRows()` does the dressing (`marketAsPick`): a row counts as a pick only while its `pickId` is on the current board; otherwise it is market information whatever the file says. Keep this rule when adding slots. Since 2026-11-10 three of those slots lead with the watchlist instead when anything is on watch (the home page's ranked list, the digest slate, and the picks page, where the best-parlays list is one sentence on a night with no lean): see The watchlist, held games and flagged leans.

## The watchlist, held games and flagged leans (2026-11-10)

The 2026-11-09 afternoon run posted no lean (20 games, 118 sides). The review of that night found the pages said little about what nearly made it, and five defects; all of it is client-side, in `js/app.js` and the pages, with no change to the pipeline or the data files.

- **The watchlist** (`EdgeLine.watchlist()`; the session's bold change, not yet reviewed by the owner): what is closest to a lean and what would change it. One entry a game, because the pipeline posts at most one lean a game: of an open game's sides on watch (`rowStatus` "watch": at least halfway to the bar; a held side past its bar counts; a side the pipeline rules out never does), the one nearest its own bar. A game that already has a lean, or has started, is left out. Each entry is `{ row, pick (dressed for `pickCard`, ids `w0`, `w1`, …), more (the game's other sides on watch), kind, price, held }`, where `kind` is `short` (under its bar; `price` is the price that clears it), `held` (past its bar in a game waiting on news) or `caps` (past its bar, kept off by one a game / ten a day / five a sport). With nothing on watch it is one line, never an empty section. index.html: with no play that has an edge (no lean and no player prop), no open lean and something on watch, the ranked list (`#board`) is "On watch, no lean yet" and lists the first four entries with what each needs (`.board-watch`, `.board-need`: the clearing price, or why it is held and when that is decided), linking to `picks.html#watchlist`; with nothing on watch the closest markets stand ("Worth a look tonight"). ## Subscriptions: the calendar and the feed (`scripts/feeds.js`)

Shipped 2026-11-04 as the session's bold change (the owner asked for one that improves conversions; not yet reviewed). The site had no way for a visitor to come back: every sign-up button was a dead `#`. The feeds are the zero-backend answer. `node scripts/feeds.js` reads `data/picks.js`, `data/market.js` and `data/picks-log.json` and writes two files at the repo root, deterministic for a given set of data files so the workflows only commit when a run changed something (both workflows run it after the pipeline and include the two files in the diff check and the commit):

- **`picks.ics`**: `VCALENDAR` with a one-hour refresh hint. One `VEVENT` per board pick, `UID` = `<pick id>@edgeline`, `DTSTART` = `commence` (UTC), three hours long, a `VALARM` 30 minutes before, `SUMMARY` = "Play of the day: " prefix when it is, then `pick odds at book · tier`, `DESCRIPTION` = the rationale, support, risks, verdict, the price ladder and the breakdown link, `LOCATION` = the venue when known. Every pick is written once from a merged record (the log entry when present, else picks.js), so a started pick on picks.js is not duplicated. Log entries from the last 30 days that are not on the board keep the **same UID**: a pick in play reads "· in play", a graded one "· Won +1.40u" / "· Lost −1.00u" / "· Push" with CLV in the description, so the calendar updates in place and becomes the public record. Void entries are left out. On a no-play night an all-day event (`slate-<ET date>@edgeline`) carries the never-empty line ("No play tonight: N sides priced in G games, none clears the bar") and the three closest sides, one per market. Lines are folded at 75 octets and text is escaped per RFC 5545 (`icsText`, `fold`).
- **`feed.xml`**: RSS 2.0 with an `atom:link rel="self"`. One item per board pick (`guid` = pick id, `pubDate` = `loggedAt`), one per parlay of the day, one per grade (`guid` = `<id>#graded`, a new item so readers notify), and one per full run (`guid` = `slate-<generatedAt>`, so hourly track runs do not add items). Newest first, slate first on a tie, 80 items at most. Descriptions are escaped HTML built from the same text lines (`html()`); never raw data.

## Second price source: SportsGameOdds (`scripts/sgo.js`)

Free "amateur" tier: 2,500 event objects a month (every event in a response costs one, usage endpoint is free), 10 requests a minute (the adapter spaces calls 6.2 s apart), leagues NBA, NFL, MLB, NHL, NCAAB, NCAAF, MLS, UCL (no EPL: the Premier League stays Odds-API-only; MLS is wired since 2026-11-05, on the `reg` period, 90 minutes and stoppage time: the three-way moneyline `points-home-reg-ml3way-home` / `points-away-reg-ml3way-away` / `points-all-reg-ml3way-draw`, de-vigged across all three, and the total `points-all-reg-ou-over` / `-under`; the total's ids are on the vendor's MLS page, the three-way ones follow its oddID pattern and are unconfirmed until a live response shows them, and a wrong id only means no odds for that pick), nine books (DraftKings, FanDuel, BetMGM, Caesars, ESPN BET, Bovada, PointsBet, Unibet; `williamhill` is folded into Caesars) and the vendor's own de-vigged `fairOdds` with `openFairOdds`/`openFairSpread`/`openFairOverUnder`. **No Pinnacle**, so it never finds edges; The Odds API keeps discovery and the anchor. What it does:

- **Board refresh** (`sgo.refresh`, in both the full run and `--track`): games already on the board are fetched by `eventIDs` in one call per league (the id is remembered as `sgoEventId`); a new game is found with a 30-minute `startsAfter/startsBefore` window around its start (which also returns the other games tipping then, each costing an entity). For the pick's side it records `sgo` {books on our exact line (title → price), n, best, consensus fair, vendor fair, open, now} and appends `sgoHistory` when anything changes. Those prices are merged into `prices` (fresher for shared books, new books added; the logged `odds`/`book` never change), so line shopping and parlays see nine books. The breakdown gets `mkt` ("The market (N books) opened X, now Y") as support or risk by direction, and a "better price available" line when one of its books beats the logged price.
- **Result** (`sgo.result` at grading, since 2026-11-08; it was `sgo.close`): the game's record after it ends gives `results.game` scores (soccer's `reg`) for a game ESPN cannot settle, and nothing else. Its odds are by then the last in-game state with no book open, so they are never a close; the close's SportsGameOdds source is our own pre-game `sgoHistory` (see Data pipeline, step 5).
- **Budget**: `sgoOk()` checks `/account/usage` once per run (free; the props calls check it too, each before its own call) and never spends into the reserve: `RULES.sgoReserve` (150), 50 in the cycle's last five days since 2026-11-08 (`sgoReserveNow`). The protected spend is board games × (2 full runs + the hourly track runs inside the 8-hour pre-game window) plus props lines on the full runs; a graded pick costs nothing since 2026-11-08 (the close is pre-game; only a game ESPN cannot settle asks for its result). The real count was 214 entities by 2026-11-07 23:51 UTC (props.js `lines.used`, a week into the month, board and NBA props together), a pace of about 920 a month; NBA lines add about 14 entities a night (420 a month) and NFL lines about 22 an NFL week (95–105 a month; see Props research and lines), so the protected month is about 1,000–1,100 of 2,500. The rest goes to the surplus spender on the hourly track runs (`sgoSurplus`; see the request budget). Since 2026-11-10 part of that spare reads NFL lines a day or two before game day (`sgoEarly`, about 120 a month; the same paragraph), and NBA lines are asked only for games still to start. Without `SGO_API_KEY` every call is skipped and nothing else changes.
- **Player props** (`PROP_STATS`, `playerIndex`, `findPlayer`, `propSnapshot`, `playerProps`, `nightEvents`, `eventFor`): every player's lines ride along in the game's event object at no extra entity cost when the `oddIDs` filter is off (`events({ allOdds: true })`). `playerProps(ev, name, index, stats)` takes the stat map (our key → the vendor's statID): the NBA's `PROP_STATS` by default, `SGO_STATS` from `scripts/nfl-props.js` for the NFL (`passing_yards`, `passing_touchdowns`, `rushing_yards`, `receiving_yards`, `receiving_receptions`: the vendor's market list, checked 2026-11-08, shows each two-sided at all six books the free tier carries; the anytime touchdown, `touchdowns-<PLAYER>-game-yn-yes`, is quoted "no" at only two of them, so it is not used; since 2026-11-10 also `passing_attempts`, `passing_completions`, `passing+rushing_yards` and `rushing_attempts`, the ids the vendor's own NFL market page lists with an over and an under each (sportsgameodds.com/blog/nfl-odds-api-market-coverage, read 2026-11-10: 39 to 41 books across its tiers); **no live response has been read for these four** (the work was done without a key), so which of the free tier's books carry each is unconfirmed until the first run's log line shows them: a market the vendor does not return has no line, and one quoted one way never reaches the board). NFL player ids look like `PATRICK_MAHOMES_1_NFL`. `update-props.js` uses this: one call per league with a start window around the slate's games (one entity per game, about 14 an NBA night) and, per player, the main line per stat (the number the most books quote on both sides, ties to the vendor's `bookOverUnder`; then `bookOverUnder`, `fairOverUnder` and the most common one-sided number as fallbacks), every book's price on that exact line for over and under, the best book per side, and the fair probability per side: the vendor's de-vigged pair when `fairOverUnder` is on that line, else our own de-vig across the books quoting both sides. Players are matched by normalised name (accents, suffixes and hyphens stripped) from the event's `players` map, or read back out of the odd ids (`LUKA_DONCIC_1_NBA`) when the map is missing; last name plus first initial is the fallback when unique.

## Props research and lines

`scripts/update-props.js` runs in the same workflow step sequence as the picks (both daily runs) and never fails the job. It runs two passes that stand alone (one failing keeps the other: a failed NFL pass keeps the last file's NFL games still ahead, a failed NBA pass keeps the last file's NBA games when the NFL pass ran): **the NFL first** (`scripts/nfl-props.js`, so its lines land before Sunday's early kickoffs), then the NBA. It also writes `data/faces.js` from the NBA pass only (see Team identity); `scripts/update-teams.js` runs right after it. The NBA pass uses ESPN's public, undocumented endpoints with no key: scoreboard (tonight's games), every team's schedule (points allowed, ranked 1 = allows most), rosters of the teams playing (headshot, position, injury status), and each player's game log. Rotation players only (14+ minutes a game), up to 9 per team, last 10 regular-season games kept. About 300-500 ESPN requests per run with a 90 ms delay.

**NFL props (2026-11-08; the owner: "I also want to work on NFL picks and its props as well").** `scripts/nfl-props.js`, ESPN with no key, every request naming us honestly (`EdgeLineAI-data/1.0 (+https://ajur21.github.io/edgeline-ai/)`, the same string as `BOT_UA` in `scripts/context.js` on main; never a browser's user agent; ESPN and the NWS both answer it, checked 2026-11-08, and a source that refuses it counts as unavailable), requests 250 ms apart, about 30-60 a run once the season's box scores are cached:
- **The slate** (`slateFor`): today's NFL games while any is still to finish (a game under way stays, like the NBA's), else the earliest day ahead in this week's or next week's scoreboard (`?seasontype=&week=&dates=<season>`; ESPN's NFL scoreboard takes no date range). The calendar comes from the dated scoreboard (`leagues[0].calendar`).
- **Box scores, cached** (`updateCache`, `data/nfl-games.json`): every finished game this season, slim (one row per player: completions, attempts, passing yards and touchdowns, interceptions, carries, rushing yards and touchdowns, targets, catches, receiving yards and touchdowns; columns in `cols`) plus a name and position per player. A week whose games are all in is marked `done` and never asked for again, so a run fetches only the games finished since the last one (about 14 a week; the season's first run backfills, at most `RULES.maxNewBoxscores` 180 a run). The Pro Bowl is skipped. The file is pipeline state like `picks-log.json`: the site never loads it; the workflow commits it with the rest of `data/`. Box scores (`summary?event=`, about 600 KB each) were chosen over the per-player game log (`site.web.api.espn.com/apis/common/v3/sports/football/nfl/athletes/{id}/gamelog`, checked 2026-11-08: one season type, one category, `names` per position such as receptions, receivingTargets, receivingYards, then rushing) because one box score serves both teams' players and the defences' allowed numbers.
- **Players**: rosters of the slate's teams (position, jersey, headshot, the roster's injury status; the `injuredReserveOrOut` group counts as out). Per team: the expected starting quarterback (the one with the most attempts over the last three games, or, when he is Out, Doubtful or on IR, the healthy quarterback with the most attempts this season), the top three backs by carries plus targets, four receivers and two tight ends by targets. Out, Doubtful, IR and suspended players are left out; Questionable stays, flagged. A quarterback's rushing is graded only when he runs (4+ carries a game). Positions for every player with real usage come from rosters (the season's first run reads up to 32), so defences are read by the position they faced.
- **Markets**: nine since 2026-11-10 (the owner: "NFL props depth"), in the page's order (`STATS`, `SHIPPED`, `STAT_LIST` = `leagues.NFL.stats`): passing yards, passing TDs, pass attempts (`att`), completions (`cmp`), passing plus rushing yards (`passRushYds`), rushing yards, carries (`car`), receiving yards, receptions; the first five shipped 2026-11-08. Each is a count we can project from usage and one the books quote over and under. Keys by position (`KEYS_BY_POS`): QB passYds, passTds, att, cmp, passRushYds (+ rushYds when he runs); RB rushYds, car, recYds, rec; WR and TE recYds, rec. A stat's key is its key everywhere in the file, the log row included (a quarterback's row carries `passRushYds`). **The rule a market ships by:** the books quote it both ways, and held out on 2026 its error is no worse than the player's season average and its P(over) scores better than 2025's over rate as a constant and than a coin flip (the numbers are under The honest check). Seven candidates were fitted on 2025; four ship. **Dropped, each with its reason in `DROPPED`** (it stays in `STATS`, so `--eval` keeps measuring it; it is not projected for the page, not asked of the vendor, on no position's list): interceptions (error 1.7% worse than the season average, though its P(over) scores well), rushing plus receiving yards (error level with the season average: 29.60 against 29.58) and a touchdown (the anytime scorer is rarely quoted both ways on the free tier, a book settles it on any touchdown while the cache keeps rushing and receiving ones, and its error is 1.0% worse). To ship one later, delete its line in `DROPPED` after a check that passes.
- **Wind** (`windAt`): outdoor U.S. stadiums only (`STADIUMS`, by the home club; domes, retractable roofs and SoFi's canopy are left out, and so is a neutral or foreign site), from the National Weather Service's hourly forecast (`api.weather.gov`, public domain, keyless; it asks for a user agent naming the caller), within 6.5 days of kickoff. Open-Meteo, which `context.js` uses for the plays, is free for non-commercial use only; the NWS has no such limit.

**The NFL grade: game data only, fitted on 2025, checked on 2026.** `projectPlayer()` (server-side; the page only reads it at the typed number): usage × efficiency × the defence. Usage is 0.1 × the last three games + 0.9 × the season (a quarterback's attempts as a starter only; a backup's first start reads 30 attempts). Efficiency is yards an attempt, touchdowns an attempt, yards a carry, catch rate and yards a target, each shrunk toward the league's for the position by 25 attempts / 300 attempts / 120 carries / 5 targets / 15 targets (`PARAMS.k`). The defence is what it allowed a game to that position (passing yards and touchdowns, rushing yards by non-quarterbacks, catches and receiving yards to receivers, tight ends or backs), shrunk toward the league by 16 games, to the power 0.75: a small effect, which is what the data supported. Then home +3% / away −3%; a missing teammate's carries (a regular now out, or gone from the roster) are 30% picked up by the rest (a missing teammate's targets: no redistribution beat none, so there is none); the usual quarterback out: receivers' yards −4%, and the backup's own projection +15% (his thin relief history reads low: 49 backup starts in 2025); Questionable −5% (a prior: ESPN keeps no injury history); wind over 12 mph: passing and receiving yards −1.2% a mph, catches −0.6%, rushing +0.4%, capped (a prior from public research, not fitted, and the reasons say so); and a level per stat (`PARAMS.scale`, 0.94-1.0: a usage sample regresses toward the mean). **The four 2026-11-10 markets** use the same parts, each setting fitted on 2025 (461 quarterback starts, 739 back-games) by `fit/fit-new.js` in `research/handoff/props-data/`: pass attempts are the starter's attempts pulled toward the league's attempts a start by six games (`PARAMS.k.attG`: a count regresses), the defence at power 1 and −1% at home; completions are those attempts times his completion rate shrunk by 400 attempts, the defence at power 1, +1% at home; carries are his carries (with the missing teammate's share), the defence at 0.75, +3% at home; passing plus rushing yards is the sum of its two projections at its own level (0.986). Their spread is read from quantiles of actual ÷ projected like the yards (`DIST`; a Poisson count was too narrow for all three counts, in sample Brier 0.213 against 0.217 for attempts, 0.219 against 0.223, 0.234 against 0.237 for carries). P(over) at any number: yards from the quantiles of actual ÷ projected on 2025 (`DIST`, by projection size: a receiver projected under 25 yards is held to 0 a fifth of the time), touchdowns and catches from a Poisson count. The page grades it the NBA's way: half our P(over), half the weighted hit rate against the number (`weightedHit`, three phantom hits and misses), the same `GRADES`. The books' price is stored and takes no part. Each stat carries `why` ("7.2 targets a game at 8.9 yards a target") and its reasons (the defence's rank for the position, home or away, wind, the backup quarterback, the injury report); `signals` are the confluences (usage up or down 20%+ over the last three, a stat up or down 25%+, a home/road split of 20%+, the defence in the top or bottom 8 for the position, a short week or a bye, wind, the backup quarterback, the injury report).

What is graded: one prop per player per game, the side the board ranked for him, which is `bestSide(p, { ctx })` of js/props-grade.js on the last lines read before his game started (his likeliest side on a line quoted both ways, with enough games logged), at that posted line, against the box score; never a price. `since`: "YYYY-MM-DD", the Eastern game day of the first graded slate. `asOf`: an ISO time, when the results were last written. `record`: `{ w, l, push }`, every graded prop since `since`. `byLeague`: `{ <"NBA" | "NFL">: { w, l, push } }`. `byGrade`: `{ <tier>: { w, l, push } }`, keyed by the tier's data name, `gradeOf(prob).tier`: "High", "Medium", "Lean" (the page shows it as Low), "Coin flip". The three count the same props, so `byLeague` and `byGrade` each add up to `record`; a void is in none of them. `slates`: `[{ league, date, w, l, push }]`, one entry per league and Eastern game day with a graded prop, newest first (`date` "YYYY-MM-DD"). `recent`: `[{ league, date, gameId, playerId, name, teamAbbr, oppAbbr, home, stat, side, line, prob, grade, rank, actual, result }]`, the rows the page lists: every graded prop of each league's most recent graded game day, and every graded prop of any game still in `games` (the page swaps a started player's as-ranked row for his result by `playerId` + `gameId`, so a game that is graded while still in the file must be here in full). Per row: `league` "NBA" or "NFL"; `date` the Eastern game day; `gameId` the ESPN event id (the player's `gameId`); `playerId` the ESPN athlete id (`players[].id`); `name`; `teamAbbr`, `oppAbbr` and `home` (true when his team was at home) for "ATL @ IND"; `stat` the stat key; `side` "over" or "under"; `line` the posted number; `prob` our probability as ranked, 0 to 1; `grade` the tier's data name as ranked; `rank` his rank on his league's board for that game day, 1 the likeliest, or null; `actual` the stat's value in the box score (points + rebounds + assists for `pra`), null when void; `result` "Win" (an over finished above the line, an under below it), "Loss", "Push" (finished on the line) or "Void" (he did not play). No price field: once a game has started a price has no use, and none takes part in a result. Absent `results`, the page shows no record, and a started game's props stand as ranked under one line that says props are not graded yet. The pipeline writes `results` from its first run: until a game is graded it is `since: null`, `asOf: null`, a record of zeros, no slates and no rows (the page then shows no record and says a started prop's result comes at the next update). `byGrade` always carries the four tiers; `byLeague` a league once it has a graded prop; a slate is listed once it has a win, loss or push.

**Graded props and the record (2026-11-10): `scripts/props-log.js`, state in `data/props-log.json`.** One prop per player per game: the side the board ranked for him, `bestSide(p, { now, ctx })` of js/props-grade.js, required in Node so the pipeline and the page use the same maths.
- **Locking** (`lock(log, data, at)`): every run takes the props file as it stands and, for each player whose game is still to start, writes (or replaces) his pending prop: stat, side, line, our probability, the tier, the board's two tie-breaks, and `linesAt`, when those lines were read. A player the board stops ranking before his game (his line pulled, or he is out of the file) is taken back out. **Nothing is done for a game that has started**, so a prop is locked on the last lines read before its start, never changes side or line afterwards, and a run that first sees a game after its start locks nothing for it. No price, book or fair is stored.
- **Grading** (`grade`): once a game has had time to end (NBA 2 hours, NFL 3: no request before) its box score settles its props. NBA: ESPN's game summary, one request a game. NFL: the cached box scores in `data/nfl-games.json`, else the summary. **Win** (an over finished above the line, an under below it), **Loss**, **Push** (finished on the line), **Void** (he did not play, or the game was called off). An NFL box score lists no one without a stat, so for a player with no row ESPN's game roster decides (`sports.core.api.espn.com/…/events/<id>/competitions/<id>/competitors/<team>/roster`, read 2026-11-10: `valid` true for a player who took the field, `didNotPlay` for one who dressed and did not): played means 0 (an under on a receiver who was never thrown to wins, an over loses; voiding those would have hidden losses), anything else Void, and a roster that cannot be read waits. A game under way waits. A prop nothing settles within 7 days is dropped, never guessed. Requests name the pipeline (`UA`).
- **The log**: every prop of the last 10 days (`RULES.keepDays`), one a line in a fixed order, and `days`, one tally per league and Eastern game day (w, l, push, void, by tier) kept for good, so the record runs from the first graded slate while the file stays under about 500 KB (about 370 bytes a prop, 110 to 130 props a day). Every step is idempotent: the same file and the same box scores leave the log byte for byte as it was, and a run that grades nothing does not rewrite data/props.js. A day's tiers are written in one fixed order (`TIERS`), whatever order the props are held in (a run holds its new props last, the file holds them sorted), so the same props always write the same bytes; a props-test check turns the list round to prove it.
- **`results`** (`results(log, data)`): the contract above. `rank` is the prop's place among all of its league's props that game day, each at the line it locked on, in the board's own order (`better`). `asOf` is when a result last changed.
- **Where it runs, and why both**: the two full runs (`run()` in `update-props.js`, after the file is built: they are the only runs that rebuild the research, so the lock sees every new line) and **every hourly track run** (`track()`, called by `update-data.js --track` after the surplus's line refresh; `data/props-log.json` is in that workflow's commit check). The hourly run matters twice: its refresh re-reads lines within three hours of a start, and those are the last lines before the game, so the lock must follow it; and a result then lands within the hour of a final, like a graded pick, instead of at the next morning's run (the owner's complaint about things that pop and go). It costs ESPN requests only (about one a finished game), no SportsGameOdds entity and no Odds API credit. Each run's log line says what is locked, what waits, what was graded and the record.
- **Seeded** (2026-11-10, `seed` in the file; the script is `research/handoff/props-data/seed-log.js`): every committed version of data/props.js (31 commits, 2026-11-04 to 2026-11-11 01:47 UTC: the seed was run again at the merge, on the freshly fetched main, with `--keep-pending`) was replayed in order through `lock()` at the time it was written, then graded from ESPN's box scores. So each seeded prop is today's `bestSide` on the last committed lines read before that game started; a file written after a start never counted for that game (two NBA games on Nov 6 and one on Nov 7 had their only lines read after the tip and are not in it; nor are the NFL games before Sunday Nov 8's late window, which had no lines). The six NBA games of Nov 10 were not over at the seed, so their 82 props went into the log pending, locked the same way (two games on the 20:40 UTC lines, four on the 01:47 UTC refresh, the last read before each start), for the pipeline to grade like any other; `--keep-pending` is only sound on a history fetched that minute, because it takes the newest committed file as the last read before any start since. **The grade (owner decision, 2026-11-04, the same evening): props and plays are graded on game data, never against other books' prices, and the page must say so.** The posted line is only the number to clear; the books' fair and prices take no part in the probability, the side or the rank. The maths is `js/props-grade.js` (`window.EdgeLineProps`; since 2026-11-10 one module for the page and the pipeline, and pure: the line, the clock and `context(PROPS_DATA)` are arguments). `sideFor(p, k, line, ctx)` grades one stat at one number: `weightedHit()` is the player's hit rate against the number over the last ten games with the last five weighted 1.0 and the next five 0.6, shrunk toward even by three phantom hits and three misses (`PHANTOM`; 3-for-3 reads 60%, not 100%); for the NBA `project()` is a projection (0.5 × L5 + 0.3 × L10 + 0.2 × season) scaled by the square root of the minutes trend (L5 ÷ season, clamped 0.7–1.3), by the opponent's points allowed relative to the league average (exponent 0.6 for points and PRA, 0.4 for threes, 0.25 for rebounds and assists; `ctx.leaguePA` is the mean across tonight's opponents, 113 without a context), −4% on a back-to-back, +2% on 3–10 days' rest, blended 15% toward the home/road split when there are 4+ games, and −5% with a wider spread on any injury listing; the probability of the over is `1 − Φ((line − proj) / sd)` with sd the player's last-ten standard deviation floored per stat (`SD_FLOOR`) and widened by a quarter. For the NFL the projection and its spread come from the pipeline (`p.proj`, above; `nflOver()` reads the quantiles or the Poisson count at the number). Our probability of the over is the mean of the two; the favoured side is whichever is above even; the grade is High ≥ 70%, Medium ≥ 62%, Low ≥ 56% (shown as "Low" since 2026-11-08, owner decision: the selections are leans, so a "Lean" badge read badly; the class stays `badge-conf-lean`, and `gradeOf` returns both the page's label and the data's tier name, "Lean"), else "Coin flip" (calibrated on the first live NBA night: 809 two-sided lines, median 57%, about 6% High, 24% Medium, 43% coin flips) (`GRADES`, reusing the confidence badge classes plus `.badge-flip`). Each side carries its reasons, each with the topic it speaks to (`key`): the hit count against the number, the projection with its inputs, and whichever of minutes (NBA), usage (NFL), matchup, rest, split, injury, wind (NFL) and the stat's own trend apply, split into for, against and neutral. `bestSide(p, { now, ctx, stat })` is the player's top side across the stats that are **boardable**: a line the books quote on both sides (`twoSided`: at least one book price over and under on that exact number; the vendor's consensus alone, or a one-sided alternate ladder, is not a line), at least `minGames(p)` logged games (NBA 4, NFL 3: a month of football) and a game still to start at `now`. `better(a, b, ctx)` is the board's order: the game day first (the site rule), then our probability, then raw L10 hit rate, then sample size. **Plays follow the same rule** since the same evening: the game model in `scripts/model.js` (see Data pipeline, step 3).

## Line shopping, live status, digest

- **Line shopping** is client-side from `prices`. `EdgeLine.lineShop(pick)` sorts every book best to worst and counts how many still leave our probability an edge of at least the pick's own bar (`EdgeLine.barOf(p)`: `p.bar` when the pick carries one, else the file's floor, 3 points, for picks posted before 2026-11-05; "clear the bar", the Lean threshold, `MARKET_DATA.threshold`); `sole` is true when exactly one book does. Card fronts show the top three prices as chips plus "+N more" and a "One book only" badge; the breakdown dialog shows the full ladder with implied, edge and a state per book, and a warning when the edge lives at one book; the table view has a Books column (`clears/total`). Book abbreviations live in `BOOK_ABBR` in `js/app.js`.
- **Live status** (`js/live.js`) runs only when `PICKS_DATA.sample` is false and the page has `[data-live]` slots. For each pick within its window (20 minutes before `commence` to 5 hours after; no `commence` = today's scoreboard) it fetches ESPN's scoreboard for that sport and ET date (one request per sport+date per tick, cached 20 s), finds the game by `context.espnEventId` or team names, and renders a pill: `LIVE · clock · AWAY a–h HOME · covering by 3.5 / needs 7 more / leading by 4 / over by 2 / on the number`, or `Final · won (unofficial)`. Polls every 60 s while a game is in progress, every 5 min otherwise, never while the tab is hidden. It never grades anything; the pipeline does. ESPN's scoreboard returns `access-control-allow-origin: *`, so no proxy is needed. Failures are silent.
- **Digest** (`digest.html`) reads the same two data files. The slate is one list in the site order (game day first, then positive edge, then likeliest) with a `.day-divider` heading where the day changes (`dayBreaks`, also written into the copied text); each row's start label ("Today · 7:00 PM ET" / "Tomorrow" / weekday) comes from `commence` and falls back to parsing the `start` label; a bare weekday in a label always means a later day. `@media print` in `css/styles.css` switches the tokens to a light palette and hides the backdrop, header, buttons and slip, so "Print or save as PDF" gives a clean one-pager. "Copy as text" writes a plain-text version (`EdgeLineDigest.asText()`) to the clipboard.

## The home page and the play of the day from any play (2026-11-11)

The owner: "make the homepage better. the UI is terrible and the picks for parlays or plays of the day can also be props. its ANY play". Their three choices: the layout "Today's card" (the play and parlay of the day open the page, then one ranked list of every play with tabs, the record, the ways in, the marketing shorter below); the best play is the likeliest to hit that has an edge at its price, a prop's chance checked against how graded props have finished; both lock at 3:30 PM ET, the morning showing a front-runner marked not final, and a play that starts before then locks early.

- **The one list (`js/plays.js`).** A play is a game lean (data/picks.js) or a player prop (data/props.js) in one shape: `{ kind: "lean" | "prop", id, sport, league, sportKey, event, home, away, gameKey, commence, day, pick, market, prob, stated, odds, book, prices, ev, tier, slipped, src }`, a prop adding `player`, `stat`, `statLabel`, `statShort`, `side`, `line`, `proj`, `l10`, `reasons`, `linesAt`. A play counts while its game is still to start and it has an edge at its price at `prob` (EV > 0). A lean's `prob` is our probability as posted; a lean that slipped under its bar since is out. A prop's is the props board's stated chance **checked**: 0.5 + keep × (stated − 0.5), where keep is the share of a stated edge over 50% the graded record has borne out, Σ(hit − 0.5) / Σ(stated − 0.5) over props decided win or loss, per league, pulled toward all leagues' until a league has a few hundred graded props (`RULES.keepPrior` 200), 0.3 with no record at all (`keepDefault`). The board states 70% and more on its High props and they hit about 55%, so a stated number is never set beside a lean's as it stands. scripts/props-log.js writes the check as `PROPS_DATA.results.check` (`{ all: { n, hit, stated, keep }, NBA: {...}, NFL: {...} }`, `checkFrom`). One prop a player: his likeliest priced side with an edge. Order: Eastern game day, then `prob`, then EV, then id (`cmp`). The parlay is the likeliest two plays from different games (`gameKey`: ESPN's event id, else league, day and home club, with the props file's games as aliases) that one book prices, each with an edge at that book's price and the ticket's EV above 0; the better payout breaks a tie between books; three legs are never likelier than two. Exports are a contract with scripts/of-day.js and the pages: add names, never rename or remove one.
- **Choosing and locking (`scripts/of-day.js`).** `node scripts/update-data.js --of-day --slot <morning|afternoon|manual>` is a step of its own in update-data.yml, after the odds run and the props refresh (a full run refreshes the props, lines included, after the leans are written, so the odds run cannot choose); no API call, and a failure is a warning that never fails the job. `choose` locks the day's play (`top`) and parlay (`parlay`) on the afternoon run (`RULES.lockUTC` 20:30 UTC: 3:30 PM ET in winter, 4:30 PM in summer), on a manual run once that time has passed, and on the morning run only for a play or a leg that starts before the lock time plus `RULES.earlyMs` (two hours: GitHub starts scheduled runs late). One play and one parlay a day, never swapped, whatever a later run finds; an earlier day's play is never chosen again. The hourly run never chooses: it grades and republishes. State in data/picks-log.json: a lean that is the play keeps `potd: <day>` and `potdAt` on its own entry; a prop that is the play or a leg gets a `type: "prop"` entry locked with its line, price, checked and stated chance, `keep` and `checkedOn`, graded by `gradeProps` from ESPN's box score through scripts/props-log.js (`gradeEntries`: Win, Loss, Push; a player who did not play voids it, as a called-off game voids a lean; no box score in time voids it too); the parlay is `type: "parlay"`, its legs by id; `type: "day"` is one marker a day (`checkedAt`, `closedAt` once the locking run has happened, the candidate counts), which is how a page tells a front-runner from a day with nothing named.
- **What picks.js carries** (`publish`, written by the full run, the hourly run and the `--of-day` step, which rewrites only these three fields): `playOfDay` (null until the play locks; a prop is `kind: "prop"` with its player, stat, side, line, `statedProb`, `keep`, `checkedOn`, `actual`), `parlayOfDay` (each leg says its `kind`, `commence`, `started`, `result`, `void`; a prop leg also its player, stat, line, `modelProb` and `actual`, since no other file on the page holds them) and `ofDay: { date, lockAt, playLocked, parlayLocked, closed, checkedAt, last: { play, parlay } }` (`last` is the last play and parlay of an earlier day, for the home page's record line). An older file with no `ofDay` still shows: its play and parlay are taken as locked.
- **The records.** `PERF_DATA.potd` counts prop plays (`props`, `recent` with each play's `kind`); the leans record (`record`, `units`, `roi`) never includes a prop. The parlay record counts parlays with prop legs. A prop is graded on game data only; its price says where to bet and whether it has an edge, as on the props page.
- **On the pages (`js/app.js`).** `EdgeLine.ofDayView()` is the one rule for what a page shows: `{ today, plays, check, front, ofDay, closed, lockAt, play, parlay, stale }`. `dayPlayCard(v)` / `dayParlayCard(v)`: what the pipeline locked (`potdCard` / `parlayCard`; a prop as the play is `propPlayCard`, with the player's face, team, side and line, the checked chance and a link to the props board); after the locking run with nothing named, a card that says none was named and why (`noneCard`); before the lock, the front-runner (the first of the ranked list, and `frontParlay`), dashed and badged "Not final", with `lockLine`: "Front-runner, not final. It locks about 3:30 PM ET, once game lines and prop lines are both in, and is never swapped after." ("The lock run is due" once the time has passed); else the nearest miss (`potdCard(null)`). A locked card says "Locked 3:41 PM ET. One a day, never swapped." An earlier day's play or parlay, once finished, leaves the card for the record's last line (`stale`). index.html and digest.html load data/props.js, js/props-grade.js and js/plays.js for it; the digest's Start here and its copied text say not final and when it locks, or that no play was named. picks.html: a parlay of the day with a prop leg is no ranked ticket of game leans, so it leads the best tickets as it was locked; the builder will not load it in part and says why, and a prop can never be a slip leg (`slipLeg`). performance.html: the play-of-the-day tile says how many of the graded ones were player props.
- **The home page (index.html).** `#plays` "Today's card": the h1 kicker with the Eastern date, the lead (one lean and one parlay a day from every game lean and player prop, the lock time from `runClock(1)`), the two cards (`#potd-host`, `#parlay-host`), the update line. `#board`, the ranked list: kind tabs (All, Game leans, Player props, with counts; `data-kind-tab`), sport chips when the list holds more than one sport (`data-sport-chip`), eight rows then "Show all" (`data-more`), day headings when it spans days; a row (`.play-row`, `data-kind`) is the rank chip, the club's logo or the player's face, the pick, a meta line (sport badge, market or "Player prop", the short event, start, tier), "% to hit" with `probMeter` (fill our chance, tick the price's), the best price and book, and the edge; it links to the lean's card or the prop's row on the props board (`propHref`). With no play that has an edge, the never-empty fallbacks in order: the open leans that slipped under their bar ("Open leans, likeliest first"; none can be the play of the day), the watchlist ("On watch, no lean yet"), the closest markets ("Worth a look tonight"), a line. Under it the "Earlier today" link and the foot: how many leans and props have an edge, the slipped leans and the priced props without an edge left out, each with the way to them. `#record`: four tiles (the lean of the day and the parlay lean of the day from `PERF_DATA`, leans of the last 30 days, player props graded from `PROPS_DATA.results`), then the last play and parlay. `#more`: three doors (the NFL week, the props board, every game) with what each holds now. Then the edge field, How it works with the calculator, Subscribe, Pricing, FAQ. The ticker carries the first twelve of the list (or the closest markets). The strip: Today's card, Ranked list, Record, NFL, props, games, Every side, How it works, Subscribe, Pricing, FAQ.
- **Copy.** Nothing says a play "beats its price": the site's word is edge (our chance minus what the price implies), so the hero, the list's foot, the FAQ and the empty states say "has an edge at its price". The body check in scripts/behavior.js refuses "beats its/their/the price" on the home page, inline scripts included (it reads `textContent`), so keep the phrase out of comments there too.
- **Tests.** scripts/plays-test.js (in `npm test`) and scripts/home-checks.js (run by scripts/behavior.js): see the file map. The sample fixtures carry the play and parlay the new rule locks on the sample day (a lean of today's games and a two-leg parlay), the lock times and `ofDay`; sample-props.js carries `results.check`; sample-performance.js the props count and recent plays. The watchlist checks serve no props, since a night with props that have an edge lists those on the home page instead.
