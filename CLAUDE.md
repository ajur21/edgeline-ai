# EdgeLine AI — agent brief

Read this first. It is the hand-off document for any agent or developer working on this site.

## What this is

A static marketing and product website for an AI sports betting picks service. Three pages, no framework, no build step. Open `index.html` in a browser or serve the folder from any static host.

"EdgeLine AI" is a placeholder brand. All copy, prices, picks and performance numbers are placeholders and are labelled "Sample data" in the UI. Do not remove those labels until real data is wired in.

## Conventions (keep these)

- **Ordering rule (owner decision, 2026-11-04, refined twice the same day): game day first, then positive edge, then likeliest.** The owner's words: "order things by date as well. no reason for a play from tomorrow to pop up before a play from today." Every list of picks or sides (hero board, ticker, top three, picks grid and table, market board, best parlays, digest top three and slate, the never-empty fallbacks) is sorted first by the Eastern calendar day of the game (`EdgeLine.dayKey(p)`: `commence` through `Intl.DateTimeFormat` in America/New_York, falling back to the `start` label the way the digest parses it, where "Today"/"Tonight" is the data's day and a bare weekday is always a later day), then in two tiers: sides with a positive edge, then everything else; within each tier by fair probability descending, with edge as the tiebreaker (`EdgeLine.sortByProb` / `worthFirst`, which compares `dayCmp` first; `marketRows` does the same for market rows, keeping the likelier side of each market; `bestParlays` orders tickets by the day of their earliest leg, then fair probability, then EV, and each ticket carries `day`). The day boundary is visible: a day heading (`.day-divider` in the picks grid and the digest slate via `EdgeLine.dayDivider(key)`, `tr.day-divider` from `marketTable({ dayDivider: true })`; text like "Tomorrow · Thu Nov 6" or "Saturday · Nov 8", built from `EdgeLine.dayLabel(key)`, which is relative to the current Eastern date) opens each day whenever the list spans more than one, on the default order and on the start-time order. Rank chips still number the whole list in display order, so #1 is today's likeliest. Real picks always have a positive edge, so the tiers only show on the fallbacks and the market board, where a dashed divider ("Priced against you from here") marks the boundary within each day that has both tiers (`marketTable({ divider })`, `.grid-divider` in the picks grid fallback). The order must be visible, not implied: a rank chip on every card and row (`EdgeLine.rankChip`, "#1 · Likeliest" on top), a probability bar on every card (fill = fair probability, tick = what the price implies, hairline = 50%), Fair as the lead metric, and a one-line "how to read" under each ranked heading. Sort controls on the picks page and the market board default to "Likeliest first"; the other orders are opt-in. The one exception is the nearest-miss slot (`potdCard(null)`, "Closest to the bar"), which is by edge by definition (`marketRows({ byEdge: true })`). Best parlays rank by the day of the earliest leg, then the ticket's fair probability, then EV; the parlay of the day (highest EV) is always shown, at its real rank. The owner also asked that pages guide the reader rather than list things: when adding a slot, say what the order is and what the numbers mean.

## Data pipeline

`scripts/update-data.js` is the "backend". `.github/workflows/track-prices.yml` runs `node scripts/update-data.js --track` **hourly for sixteen hours**, 10 AM to 1 AM Eastern (15:00-06:00 UTC): no Odds API requests, it refreshes DraftKings' open and current numbers from ESPN for every board pick and, for games starting within `sgoTrackWindowHours` (8), every book's price from SportsGameOdds, rebuilds the breakdowns, rewrites `data/picks.js` (keeping `generatedAt`, adding `trackedAt`) and commits only when a number moved. Cost: ESPN is free and unmetered; SportsGameOdds spends one entity per board game per hourly run inside the 8-hour window (roughly 6 games × 8 runs = 48 a day on a busy board, about 1,450 a month, plus the full runs and closes), with `sgoReserve` as the backstop. The full odds run cannot go hourly on The Odds API's free tier (3 credits per sport per run; two runs a day is the ceiling). `.github/workflows/update-data.yml` runs the full pipeline twice a day, 17:00 UTC (around noon Eastern) and 20:30 UTC (late afternoon, before the evening slates), and on demand, then commits `data/` and redeploys. A 4 AM run found no edges; the two-run schedule is an experiment, and each logged pick records `loggedAt` so the log shows which run produces edges. Drop the weaker one later. It needs the `ODDS_API_KEY` repository secret (a key from https://the-odds-api.com; the free tier is 500 requests/month).

How a pick is made (all in `update-data.js`, tunable in `RULES`):
1. For each active sport (on its game days: NBA/NHL/MLB daily, NFL Wed/Sat/Sun/Mon, Premier League Fri-Sun; La Liga is switched off to stay inside the free tier), fetch moneyline, spread and total prices for ten named bookmakers: Pinnacle plus nine bettable U.S. books (ten bookmakers cost the same as one region: 3 requests per sport, 2 for soccer).
2. Per event and market, group books by exact line and keep the largest group (at least 3 books). The **fair** probability is Pinnacle's de-vigged price for that line (the sharp reference). If Pinnacle posts a neighbouring line instead (NFL and NBA spreads and totals only, at most `maxAnchorShift` = 1 point away), its de-vigged price is **carried** to the books' line at a per-sport slope (`LINE_VALUE`: NFL 2.2%/pt spreads, 1.5%/pt totals; NBA 2.8%/pt and 1.4%/pt), the two sides renormalised to one; the pick records `anchorShift` and an `anchorNote`, the rationale and breakdown say so, and the breakdown files it under notes rather than support ("a fair estimate, not a quote"). Hockey, baseball and soccer lines move in steps too big to convert. Only when neither applies does the de-vigged average of the other books stand in. The site calls this "Fair"; the data field is still `modelProb`.
3. For each outcome, take the best price among the bettable books (never Pinnacle). Edge = fair − implied(best price). Edge of 1 point or more becomes a pick (Lean; about +2% expected value at even odds); 2 is Medium; 3.5 is High. Each pick records `ev` (expected profit per unit). Only one side of a market can be picked.
4. At most 4 picks per sport and 12 per run; only events starting within 36 hours. A pick is logged once per event+market+outcome+line, at the price seen. On every later run before tip-off, `priceOutcomes()` prices **every** outcome on every line two or more books share (not just the main line, not just positive edges) so each pending pick's `current` price and `history` keep updating even after its edge is gone or its line is no longer the main one. That is what makes the close real.
5. Pending picks whose events started 3+ hours ago are graded. Scores come from free sources first: ESPN's scoreboard for the game's ET date (already fetched for context), then SportsGameOdds' results; **The Odds API scores endpoint is only called for a sport with picks neither could settle**, which cuts its credit use by roughly a third. Win pays the profit at the logged odds for 1 unit; loss is −1; push 0. Ungraded after 7 days becomes `void` and is excluded. At grading, `close` and `clv` are written. The close comes first from **SportsGameOdds** (`close.source` = "consensus close (SportsGameOdds, N books)"): the vendor's de-vigged fair at its closing line when that line is ours (always for a moneyline), else our own de-vig across the books still on our line; then from **ESPN's core odds record** for the game (`sports.core.api.espn.com/.../odds/100`, DraftKings, free, no key): after a game it holds `open`, `current` and `close` per side, so every matched pick gets a true closing number regardless of when we last ran (`close.source` = "DraftKings close (ESPN)"); failing that, the last Odds API history point before tip-off ("last check (Odds API)"); failing both, null. **CLV** = closing fair probability (DraftKings' two-way price de-vigged, or our fair at the last check) − implied probability of the logged price, in probability points; positive means the price we took beat the close. For spreads and totals the price CLV is only computed when the close is on our number; otherwise `clv` is null and `lineMove` says whether the line moved `toward` or `away` from the pick (spread: a worse number for our side is toward; total: up for an over, down for an under). Draws have no ESPN close (two-way block only).
6. `performance.js` summarises graded picks from the last 30 days by event date (Eastern), plus `byDate` (all-time daily profit for the calendar), `potd` and `parlays` records, and `clv` (`n` picks with price CLV, `avg` in points, `beat`, `beatPct`, `graded`, `lineToward`, `lineAway`, `espnCloses`); each `recent` row carries `clv`, `closeOdds`, `closeLine`, `closeSource` and `lineMove`. `picks.js` is every pending pick whose event is still ahead, each with `prices` (price at every bettable book on that line), `books`, `anchor`, `ev`, `outcome`, `commence` and `sportKey` (the digest orders by `commence`; live status needs `sportKey`, `commence`, `outcome` and `context.espnEventId`).
7. **Play of the day** (`playOfDay` in picks.js): the highest-EV pick on the board, chosen once per Eastern day at the first run that has picks (`potd` date on the log entry) and never swapped that day. **Parlay of the day** (`parlayOfDay`): 2-3 legs from different games, all +EV at one book, the combination with the highest EV (fair = product of fair probabilities, treating legs as independent); logged as a `type: "parlay"` entry with `legPrices`, one per day. Parlays grade once every leg settles: any loss loses, pushes drop out, the rest pay the combined price; a void leg voids it.
8. **Breakdowns.** After the board is built, `scripts/context.js` enriches every pending pick (free sources, each optional): ESPN injuries for both teams (status, type, return date, note), the ESPN scoreboard for that date (venue, indoor flag, records, probable pitchers, TV, ESPN's own weather, and the DraftKings odds block: open and current spread, total and moneyline, stored as `context.odds`; the pick's own side is summarised in `dk` {open, now, moved, label} and appended to `dkHistory` whenever it changes, up to 48 points), each team's schedule (rest days, back-to-back, games in the last 7 days), and Open-Meteo wind/rain/temperature at kickoff for outdoor games (city geocoded from the venue). The pipeline also records every price it sees for a logged pick (`current`, `history`) so a breakdown can show line movement. `buildBreakdown()` writes: the tier and exactly why (edge vs the 1 / 2 / 3.5 point thresholds; confidence is the price edge only), the price rationale, "what supports it", "what could go wrong", notes and a one-line verdict. Rules of thumb: injuries on the pick's team are risks and on the opponent support; for totals, players out lean under; back-to-backs lean under and against the tired side; wind 15+ mph, rain 50%+ or freezing lean under; a sharp-line move of 1+ point toward or away is support or risk; edge gone at current prices is a risk; DraftKings' number moving toward the pick since the open is support, against it a risk (shown in the dialog's Line movement cell with a ▲/▼ tag). "Benching" and rest decisions surface through the injury report's status/type (e.g. "Out, Rest"); there is no separate lineup feed. The site shows all of this in the Full breakdown dialog (`EdgeLine.openBreakdown`), reachable from every card back, the play of the day and each parlay's legs.
9. The picks page lists the best parlays client-side: `EdgeLine.bestParlays` enumerates every 2-4 leg combination of distinct games that is +EV at a single book, keeps the best book per leg set, and ranks by EV. The parlay builder re-uses `prices` the same way: `EdgeLine.parlayMath` prices a slip at one book (auto-best, chosen, or mixed best prices with a warning). The slip lives in localStorage (`parlay-slip`, `parlay-book`).

Request budget: 3 per sport fetched (2 for soccer) plus 2 per sport with pending picks for grading, twice a day: roughly 450-480 a month against the free tier's 500. Adding La Liga or a third run needs the paid tier. The script stops fetching odds when the API reports fewer than 25 requests left. `node scripts/update-data.js --verbose --out /tmp/x` prints every line's best edge (costs real requests). Sample data stays in place (flagged `sample: true`) until the first real run; the UI hides "Sample" labels when `sample` is false.

To run locally: `ODDS_API_KEY=... npm run data`. To test without the network: `npm test` (uses `scripts/fixtures/`).

## The market board and the never-empty rule

Every full run writes `data/market.js`: both sides of every main-line market it priced (`priceOutcomes` rows with `qualifies`), with event, start label, best price and book, every book's price, fair (`modelProb`), edge, EV, anchor and `anchorShift`, flagged `status`: `pick` (a pending pick on the board, with `pickId`), `watch` (edge of at least `RULES.watchEdge` = half a point, never a pick) or `none`. Sorted by edge; `--track` runs leave it alone. The picks page shows it as the **Market board** (`#market`): summary line, sport / market / status / search filters, 40 rows at a time with a pick row linking to its card.

**Owner rule (2026-11-04): nothing on the site stays empty.** When no pick clears the bar, every slot falls back to the closest markets, always labelled Watch / No bet and never dressed as a play: the home ticker and hero board ("Closest to the bar"), the play-of-the-day slot (`potdCard(null)` shows the nearest miss with a note), the parlay slot and the best-parlays list (`pulseCard`: games, markets, watch count, top three), the top-three grid and the picks grid (market cards: status badge instead of a confidence badge, no parlay button, a Market board link instead of Full breakdown), and the digest (lede, tiles, top three, slate retitled "The watch list"). `EdgeLine.marketRows()` does the dressing (`marketAsPick`): a row counts as a pick only while its `pickId` is on the current board; otherwise it is market information whatever the file says. Keep this rule when adding slots.

## Team identity: logos, matchups and faces (`js/teams.js`)

Shipped 2026-11-04 as the session's bold change (the owner asked for faces and logos taken from ESPN; not yet reviewed). The site had no visual identity per game: every card was a sport badge and two lines of text. Now:

- **Resolver.** `EdgeLineTeams.find(sportOrKey, name)` maps the odds feed's names ("Los Angeles Clippers", "LA Dodgers", "NY Rangers", "Buffalo", "Man City") to a record in `data/teams.js` through a per-league alias index (display name, short name, nickname, location, abbreviation, "LA"/"NY"/"GS"/"SF"/"TB"/"NE"/"KC" forms) with a unique partial match as the fallback; an alias two clubs share ("New York") resolves to null, never to the wrong club. `side(p)` says whether a pick is on the home or away team, a total or a draw, from `outcome.name` or the pick text; `sides(p)` parses "Away @ Home" / "Home vs. Away" when a parlay leg only carries the event label.
- **Logos.** `logo(team, name, px)` is an `<img>` from ESPN's CDN through its resizer (`combiner/i?img=...&w=&h=`, about 5 KB instead of 60) that swaps itself for a monogram chip (`.team-mono`, the abbreviation or initials) on error, so a blocked CDN or a missing file never shows a broken image. Sizes 20/24/28/40/72 are styled by the `width` attribute.
- **Matchups.** `matchup(p, { size, abbr })` renders away @ home (soccer: "v"); the picked side gets `.is-pick` (a ring and glow in `--team`, the club's primary colour from the data), the other side `.is-off` (dimmed, desaturated); a total lights both with no ring. It sits on every pick card front, the play of the day (large), parlay and pulse legs, the hero board and ticker (`sideLogo`: the picked club alone, or the small matchup for totals), the market board's Game cell, the picks table, the digest's top three and slate rows, and the breakdown dialog head. `tint(card, p)` sets `--team` and `.has-team` on a card for the faint colour wash in its top corner.
- **Faces.** `faces(p, { perTeam, px, title })` reads `data/faces.js` (NBA only: `facesFrom()` in `update-props.js` writes every rotation player with season points and minutes plus everyone ESPN lists as Out, who are otherwise skipped before the game-log fetch) and lists up to three per team: Out first, then day-to-day by scoring, then the top scorers, each with the ESPN headshot (`headshot(sportKey, id)`) or initials. Shown on the card back under the factors and in the breakdown as "Names to know". The breakdown's injury report also shows a face per listed player: `context.js` now records `athleteId` and `headshot` on each injury.
- **Rules.** Everything degrades to the previous text-only rendering when `js/teams.js` or its data files are missing (`TM()` in app.js checks). No new colour tokens: `--team` comes from data and every rule falls back to `--accent`. The market board keeps its ten columns inside 1280px (22px logos, no "@", 8px cell padding; a behaviour check measures it). Attribution line in every footer: "Team logos and player photos via ESPN." Licensing is the props-page caveat: fine for a demo, licensed before sale.

## Subscriptions: the calendar and the feed (`scripts/feeds.js`)

Shipped 2026-11-04 as the session's bold change (the owner asked for one that improves conversions; not yet reviewed). The site had no way for a visitor to come back: every sign-up button was a dead `#`. The feeds are the zero-backend answer. `node scripts/feeds.js` reads `data/picks.js`, `data/market.js` and `data/picks-log.json` and writes two files at the repo root, deterministic for a given set of data files so the workflows only commit when a run changed something (both workflows run it after the pipeline and include the two files in the diff check and the commit):

- **`picks.ics`**: `VCALENDAR` with a one-hour refresh hint. One `VEVENT` per board pick, `UID` = `<pick id>@edgeline`, `DTSTART` = `commence` (UTC), three hours long, a `VALARM` 30 minutes before, `SUMMARY` = "Play of the day: " prefix when it is, then `pick odds at book · tier`, `DESCRIPTION` = the rationale, support, risks, verdict, the price ladder and the breakdown link, `LOCATION` = the venue when known. Log entries from the last 30 days that are not on the board keep the **same UID**: a pick in play reads "· in play", a graded one "· Won +1.40u" / "· Lost −1.00u" / "· Push" with CLV in the description, so the calendar updates in place and becomes the public record. Void entries are left out. On a no-play night an all-day event (`slate-<ET date>@edgeline`) carries the never-empty line ("No play tonight: N sides priced in G games, none clears the bar") and the three closest sides, one per market. Lines are folded at 75 octets and text is escaped per RFC 5545 (`icsText`, `fold`).
- **`feed.xml`**: RSS 2.0 with an `atom:link rel="self"`. One item per board pick (`guid` = pick id, `pubDate` = `loggedAt`), one per parlay of the day, one per grade (`guid` = `<id>#graded`, a new item so readers notify), and one per full run (`guid` = `slate-<generatedAt>`, so hourly track runs do not add items). Newest first, slate first on a tie, 80 items at most. Descriptions are escaped HTML built from the same text lines (`html()`); never raw data.
- The site: `index.html#subscribe` (between the plays and how it works) shows the next calendar event built client-side from the same data (the earliest pick on the board, or the no-play all-day event with the closest side), two channel cards with "Add to calendar" (`webcal://` link, rewritten from `location` when served over http(s), else the `data-site` attribute), "Open the feed", and copy-link buttons; every page's `<head>` has the `rel="alternate"` RSS link and the footer has a Subscribe link; the Free plan's button points at the section; the digest has a one-line pointer under its tiles. The sample badge in the section follows `data-sample-flag="picks"`.
- Base URL: `DEFAULT_BASE` in `scripts/feeds.js` and the `data-site` / `webcal` attributes in `index.html` carry the GitHub Pages URL. Change all three when the domain changes. Calendar clients refetch on their own schedule (Apple and Outlook roughly hourly, Google up to a day), so the feed is the faster channel.

## Second price source: SportsGameOdds (`scripts/sgo.js`)

Free "amateur" tier: 2,500 event objects a month (every event in a response costs one, usage endpoint is free), 10 requests a minute (the adapter spaces calls 6.2 s apart), leagues NBA, NFL, MLB, NHL, NCAAB, NCAAF, MLS, UCL (no EPL: soccer stays Odds-API-only), nine books (DraftKings, FanDuel, BetMGM, Caesars, ESPN BET, Bovada, PointsBet, Unibet; `williamhill` is folded into Caesars) and the vendor's own de-vigged `fairOdds` with `openFairOdds`/`openFairSpread`/`openFairOverUnder`. **No Pinnacle**, so it never finds edges; The Odds API keeps discovery and the anchor. What it does:

- **Board refresh** (`sgo.refresh`, in both the full run and `--track`): games already on the board are fetched by `eventIDs` in one call per league (the id is remembered as `sgoEventId`); a new game is found with a 30-minute `startsAfter/startsBefore` window around its start (which also returns the other games tipping then, each costing an entity). For the pick's side it records `sgo` {books on our exact line (title → price), n, best, consensus fair, vendor fair, open, now} and appends `sgoHistory` when anything changes. Those prices are merged into `prices` (fresher for shared books, new books added; the logged `odds`/`book` never change), so line shopping and parlays see nine books. The breakdown gets `mkt` ("The market (N books) opened X, now Y") as support or risk by direction, and a "better price available" line when one of its books beats the logged price.
- **Close** (`sgo.close` at grading): the game's record after it ends still carries every book's last price and the vendor fair, plus `results.game` scores. Preferred over ESPN's DraftKings close.
- **Budget**: `RULES.sgoReserve` (150) entities are never touched; `sgoOk()` checks `/account/usage` once per run. Rough spend: board games × (2 full runs + up to 8 hourly track runs inside the 8-hour pre-game window) plus one per graded pick, about 1,500–2,000 a month on a busy board. Without `SGO_API_KEY` every call is skipped and nothing else changes.
- **Player props** (`PROP_STATS`, `playerIndex`, `findPlayer`, `propSnapshot`, `playerProps`, `nightEvents`, `eventFor`): every player's lines ride along in the game's event object at no extra entity cost when the `oddIDs` filter is off (`events({ allOdds: true })`). `update-props.js` uses this: one call with a start window around tonight's games (one entity per game, about 14 an NBA night) and, per player, the main line per stat (the number the most books quote on both sides, ties to the vendor's `bookOverUnder`; then `bookOverUnder`, `fairOverUnder` and the most common one-sided number as fallbacks), every book's price on that exact line for over and under, the best book per side, and the fair probability per side: the vendor's de-vigged pair when `fairOverUnder` is on that line, else our own de-vig across the books quoting both sides. Players are matched by normalised name (accents, suffixes and hyphens stripped) from the event's `players` map, or read back out of the odd ids (`LUKA_DONCIC_1_NBA`) when the map is missing; last name plus first initial is the fallback when unique.

## Props research and lines

`scripts/update-props.js` runs in the same workflow step sequence as the picks (both daily runs) and never fails the job. It also writes `data/faces.js` (see Team identity); `scripts/update-teams.js` runs right after it. It uses ESPN's public, undocumented endpoints with no key: scoreboard (tonight's games), every team's schedule (points allowed, ranked 1 = allows most), rosters of the teams playing (headshot, position, injury status), and each player's game log. Rotation players only (14+ minutes a game), up to 9 per team, last 10 regular-season games kept. About 300-500 ESPN requests per run with a 90 ms delay.

**Lines (shipped 2026-11-04 as the session's bold change; the owner asked to "put the props page together").** After the ESPN pass, `attachLines()` makes one SportsGameOdds call for the night (see Second price source: player props) and writes `p.lines` per player and `data.lines` as the summary. Budget: it skips when `SGO_API_KEY` is missing, when `--no-lines` is passed, or when the month's remaining entities minus tonight's games would dip under `RULES.sgoReserve` (150, the same backstop as the board). The workflow passes `--no-lines` on the noon run and spends on the afternoon run and manual runs (`props_only` input: props, lines and team identity only, no Odds API credits), so lines cost about 14 entities a night, roughly 420 a month on top of the board's spend. Lines are posted by most books in the afternoon; the noon run would mostly find nothing.

**The grade (owner decision, 2026-11-04, the same evening): props and plays are graded on game data, never against other books' prices, and the page must say so.** The posted line is only the number to clear; the books' fair and prices take no part in the probability, the side or the rank. Client-side in `props.html`, `sideFor(p, k, line)` grades one stat at one number: `weightedHit()` is the player's hit rate against the number over the last ten games with the last five weighted 1.0 and the next five 0.6, shrunk toward even by three phantom hits and three misses (`PHANTOM`; 3-for-3 reads 60%, not 100%); `project()` is a projection (0.5 × L5 + 0.3 × L10 + 0.2 × season) scaled by the square root of the minutes trend (L5 ÷ season, clamped 0.7–1.3), by the opponent's points allowed relative to the league average (exponent 0.6 for points and PRA, 0.4 for threes, 0.25 for rebounds and assists; `LEAGUE_PA` is the mean across tonight's opponents), −4% on a back-to-back, +2% on 3–10 days' rest, blended 15% toward the home/road split when there are 4+ games, and −5% with a wider spread on any injury listing; the probability of the over is `1 − Φ((line − proj) / sd)` with sd the player's last-ten standard deviation floored per stat (`SD_FLOOR`) and widened by a quarter. Our probability of the over is the mean of the two; the favoured side is whichever is above even; the grade is High ≥ 70%, Medium ≥ 62%, Lean ≥ 56%, else "Coin flip" (calibrated on the first live night: 809 two-sided lines, median 57%, about 6% High, 24% Medium, 43% coin flips) (`GRADES`, reusing the confidence badge classes plus `.badge-flip`). Each side carries its reasons: the hit count against the number, the projection with its inputs, and whichever of minutes, matchup, rest, split, injury and the stat's own trend apply, split into for / "But:" against / neutral. `bestSide(p)` is the player's top side across the stats that are **boardable**: a line the books quote on both sides (`twoSided`: at least one book price over and under on that exact number; the vendor's consensus alone, or a one-sided alternate ladder, is not a line) and at least `MIN_GAMES` (4) logged games; a card still grades a thinner sample or a typed number and lists "Small sample" among the reasons. The **props board** lists every best side in the filtered list sorted `better()`: our probability, then raw L10 hit rate, then sample size; no tier or divider; the row shows the side, line and stat, the projection, the best price only as "where to bet it", our probability with the bar (tick = raw hit rate), the hit pill, the grade badge and the for/against counts; ten rows then "Show all"; "Open card" scrolls to the card, switches it to that stat and focuses the line box. The rank carries onto the card as a chip beside the name. Each card opens on its ranked stat with the posted line typed in (stat chips show the line), two side pills (our probability per side, best price and book, the favoured side marked) with the grade badge, the hit pill on the favoured side, and a "Why over/under" list under the chart; typing any other number regrades everything from the game data for that number, with a "Market line" reset. Overrides are stored as `{ v, m }` (value and the posted line it was typed over) so a stale override gives way to a fresh line. Sort defaults to "Likeliest first" (the board's order; unpriced players after it by heat), with "Form" (hit rate), hottest, season average, minutes and "Most reasons" as opt-ins; without any lines the board is hidden, the two sorts are disabled, "Hottest" is the default and every card still grades a typed line the same way. The how-to under the board, the rule line under the title, the board summary and the notice all state that the grade is game data only and not other books' prices. **Plays are not yet on this rule**: their confidence tier is still the price edge against the sharp anchor (see Data pipeline); moving them to a game-data grade is backlog item 3f.

Signals (`signals()` in the script) are confluences, not bets: a stat up or down 12%+ over the last 5 vs the season, minutes up or down 10%+, a home/road scoring split of 12%+, back-to-back or 3+ days' rest, opponent in the top 8 or bottom 8 for points allowed, a steady scorer (last-10 standard deviation under 22% of the average), and any injury listing. The page lets the visitor type a line per stat and shows over hit rates for L5, L10 and the season; typed lines are kept in localStorage.

**Licensing:** ESPN's API is unofficial and its photos are not licensed for commercial use; the page credits ESPN and SportsGameOdds and the stats and photos must move to a licensed provider (SportsDataIO, Sportradar) before this is sold.

## Line shopping, live status, digest

- **Line shopping** is client-side from `prices`. `EdgeLine.lineShop(pick)` sorts every book best to worst and counts how many still leave a 1-point edge ("clear the bar", the Lean threshold); `sole` is true when exactly one book does. Card fronts show the top three prices as chips plus "+N more" and a "One book only" badge; the breakdown dialog shows the full ladder with implied, edge and a state per book, and a warning when the edge lives at one book; the table view has a Books column (`clears/total`). Book abbreviations live in `BOOK_ABBR` in `js/app.js`.
- **Live status** (`js/live.js`) runs only when `PICKS_DATA.sample` is false and the page has `[data-live]` slots. For each pick within its window (20 minutes before `commence` to 5 hours after; no `commence` = today's scoreboard) it fetches ESPN's scoreboard for that sport and ET date (one request per sport+date per tick, cached 20 s), finds the game by `context.espnEventId` or team names, and renders a pill: `LIVE · clock · AWAY a–h HOME · covering by 3.5 / needs 7 more / leading by 4 / over by 2 / on the number`, or `Final · won (unofficial)`. Polls every 60 s while a game is in progress, every 5 min otherwise, never while the tab is hidden. It never grades anything; the pipeline does. ESPN's scoreboard returns `access-control-allow-origin: *`, so no proxy is needed. Failures are silent.
- **Digest** (`digest.html`) reads the same two data files. The slate is one list in the site order (game day first, then positive edge, then likeliest) with a `.day-divider` heading where the day changes (`dayBreaks`, also written into the copied text); each row's start label ("Today · 7:00 PM ET" / "Tomorrow" / weekday) comes from `commence` and falls back to parsing the `start` label; a bare weekday in a label always means a later day. `@media print` in `css/styles.css` switches the tokens to a light palette and hides the backdrop, header, buttons and slip, so "Print or save as PDF" gives a clean one-pager. "Copy as text" writes a plain-text version (`EdgeLineDigest.asText()`) to the clipboard.

## Journal

`journal.html` is a personal bet log stored in IndexedDB on the visitor's device (entries with screenshot blobs). Nothing is uploaded. Export/import is a JSON file with images inlined as data URLs. It shares the calendar and stat tile components with the performance page. Accounts with sync are a backlog item.
