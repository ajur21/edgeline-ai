# EdgeLine AI — agent brief

Read this first. It is the hand-off document for any agent or developer working on this site.

## What this is

A static marketing and product website for an AI sports betting picks service. Three pages, no framework, no build step. Open `index.html` in a browser or serve the folder from any static host.

"EdgeLine AI" is a placeholder brand. All copy, prices, picks and performance numbers are placeholders and are labelled "Sample data" in the UI. Do not remove those labels until real data is wired in.

## Conventions (keep these)

- **Ordering rule (owner decision, 2026-11-04, refined twice the same day): game day first, then positive edge, then likeliest.** The owner's words: "order things by date as well. no reason for a play from tomorrow to pop up before a play from today." Every list of picks or sides (hero board, ticker, top three, picks grid and table, market board, best parlays, digest top three and slate, the never-empty fallbacks) is sorted first by the Eastern calendar day of the game (`EdgeLine.dayKey(p)`: `commence` through `Intl.DateTimeFormat` in America/New_York, falling back to the `start` label the way the digest parses it, where "Today"/"Tonight" is the data's day and a bare weekday is always a later day), then in two tiers: sides with a positive edge, then everything else (a locked pick that has slipped under its bar sits between the two: see Locked picks under The per-side bar on the page); within each tier by our probability (`modelProb`, from the game model) descending, with edge as the tiebreaker (`EdgeLine.sortByProb` / `worthFirst`, which compares `dayCmp` first; `marketRows` does the same for market rows, keeping the likelier side of each market; `bestParlays` orders tickets by the day of their earliest leg, then the ticket’s probability (its legs’ probabilities multiplied), then EV, and each ticket carries `day`). The day boundary is visible: a day heading (`.day-divider` in the picks grid and the digest slate via `EdgeLine.dayDivider(key)`, `tr.day-divider` from `marketTable({ dayDivider: true })`; text like "Tomorrow · Thu Nov 6" or "Saturday · Nov 8", built from `EdgeLine.dayLabel(key)`, which is relative to the current Eastern date) opens each day whenever the list spans more than one, on the default order and on the start-time order. Rank chips still number the whole list in display order, so #1 is today's likeliest. Real picks always have a positive edge, so the tiers only show on the fallbacks and the market board, where a dashed divider ("Priced against you from here") marks the boundary within each day that has both tiers (`marketTable({ divider })`, `.grid-divider` in the picks grid fallback). The order must be visible, not implied: a rank chip on every card and row (`EdgeLine.rankChip`, "#1 · Likeliest" on top), a probability bar on every card (fill = our probability, tick = what the price implies, hairline = 50%), “To hit” (our probability) as the lead metric, and a one-line "how to read" under each ranked heading. Sort controls on the picks page and the market board default to "Likeliest first"; the other orders are opt-in. The one exception is the nearest-miss slot (`potdCard(null)`, "Closest to the bar"), which is by points past its own bar by definition (`marketRows({ byPast: true })`: edge − the row's bar, so a sport with a wide bar is not flattered by a raw edge; `byEdge` remains for callers that want raw edge). Best parlays rank by the day of the earliest leg, then the ticket’s probability, then EV; the parlay of the day (highest EV) is always shown, at its real rank. The owner also asked that pages guide the reader rather than list things: when adding a slot, say what the order is and what the numbers mean.
- **A pick's life on the page (owner complaint, 2026-11-05: picks "pop and go or stay past tip off"). Nothing looks bettable after its start, and nothing silently vanishes.** One clock in `js/app.js`: `EdgeLine.now()` (the visitor's clock; `window.EDGELINE_NOW` in ms pins it for tests) and `EdgeLine.gameState(p)`: `upcoming`, `soon` (starts within 90 minutes), `live` (started, no result, inside the sport's usual length: NBA 2.5 h, NFL 3.5 h, NHL 3 h, MLB 3.5 h, soccer 2 h), `awaiting` (past it, no result yet) or `final` (a result, or status graded / void); a pick the pipeline marks `started` is never open, whatever the visitor's clock says. `EdgeLine.isOpen(p)` / `openOnly(list)` = upcoming or soon. Every bettable surface shows open items only: the hero board, ticker, top three, picks grid and table, best parlays (`bestParlays` skips started legs), the parlay slip (a started leg is removed with a note naming it; a started pick cannot be added), the play-of-the-day button, the digest's top three, slate and lede counts, the market board (with a line counting the sides of games that have started), the edge field, the Games page cells, search suggestions and every never-empty fallback. Started items move instead: the picks page's **In progress and results** (`#played`, a strip link; live, then awaiting, then graded, with rank chips, a state chip (Live, Awaiting result, Won, Lost, Push, Void) and the live slot or the final score and units), the home page's "Earlier today" line under the board, the digest's block of the same name (also in Copy as text), the breakdown dialog's state, and the Games page's groups: **Live now**, **Starting soon**, **Later**, **Finished** (collapsed; a `#g-` link opens it); a started game shows "Lines closed at the start" and our pick with its live slot or result, and rank chips number only the games that can still be bet. A 60-second timer (and the tab becoming visible) re-renders whatever changed state. An update line sits on home, picks and digest ("Leans published 9:04 AM ET | Updated 3:05 PM ET (12 min ago) | Next check about 4:00 PM ET", from `generatedAt`, `trackedAt` and the workflow schedule) and on Games ("Lines priced … | Next pricing about …", since its prices change only on the full runs). Posted against now on cards, the play of the day and the breakdown: "Posted -108 at FanDuel", then "now -103 at FanDuel | Clears its bar to -133" from the pipeline's `valueTo` (it said "Worth a bet to" until 2026-11-05), in orange once the current price is past it. Two tokens joined every theme block and the print block: `--accent-2-text` (the Live chip) and `--on-bad` (the Lost chip's ink). The behaviour suite pins every sample context's clock to Wed Nov 4, 4:45 PM ET (`page.addInitScript`), and the sample picks carry `commence`, the lifecycle fields, one live pick (p13) and one graded pick (p14).
- **Leans, not picks (owner decision, 2026-11-05).** A held-out replay of the game model over past seasons, priced at ESPN's closing odds, lost money in every league (NHL −4.8% ± 3.7 over 883 selections, NBA −7.9% ± 2.3 over 2,494, NFL −9.5% ± 7.1 over 253; see The model against the market), so the pages, the digest and the feeds call our selections **leans** ("model leans" where the context needs it): "Today’s leans" in the nav, "Model leans for today’s slate", "All leans, likeliest first", "Lean of the day", "Parlay lean of the day", "No lean tonight" in the feeds. The home hero is "Leans from game data, likeliest first." and says what the site is: a game-data model’s leans, ranked by our probability, every result graded in public. No copy claims an edge over the books or that a side is "worth a bet" (the sort reads "Likeliest first", the price line "Clears its bar to"; the FAQ says the replay lost at the close). The replay sentence ("In a replay of past seasons these leans did not beat the closing price (NHL −4.8%, NBA −7.9%, NFL −9.5% ROI at the close). They become picks when the live record beats the close.") is a `[data-replay]` slot filled by `replayNotes()` from `EdgeLine.replayText()`: under the picks page head, in the home page’s How it works, at the foot of the digest (and in its copied text), and under the performance page head with `data-replay="detail"` (adds the ± and the count); the feeds’ slate and channel descriptions carry it too. The numbers live in one hand-written file, `data/replay.js` (`window.REPLAY_DATA` { asOf, call, leagues: [{ league, seasons, games, picks, roiClose, se }] }); every page loads it before `js/app.js` and works without it (the HTML sentence keeps its words without the figures). **The one switch is `call` in that file**: "leans" (the default, also when the file is missing) or "picks", which restores the pick wording everywhere. Generated copy asks `EdgeLine.word(k)` / `nWord(n)` (`one`, `many`, `One`, `Many`, `ofDay`, `parlayOfDay`); static copy carries the lean wording with the pick wording in a `data-pick` attribute that `applyCall()` swaps in (put it only on an element with no child elements); `scripts/feeds.js` reads `replay.js` from its data directory. Flipping it is the owner’s call, made when the live record beats the close; nothing automates it. File names, URLs, ids, data fields and anchors keep "pick" (picks.html, #parlays, #top-picks, `PICKS_DATA`, `p.pick`, picks.ics, feed guids). The Pro and Elite plans keep their sample labels and promise no wins. **The lowest confidence tier shows as "Low"** (owner decision, 2026-11-08: a "Lean" badge on a lean read badly). The pipeline still writes `confidence: "Lean"` and the badge class stays `.badge-conf-lean`; every page reads the name through `EdgeLine.tierLabel(c)` (badges on cards, the table, the breakdown head and its "Why" heading, the digest badge, tile and copied text, the home calendar card, search, the bar sentence, the calculator verdict) and the pipeline's older breakdown sentences ("Lean starts at 3.0 points") through `EdgeLine.tierText(s)`; `scripts/feeds.js` has its own `tierLabel`. The noun stays: a market row on the board carries a status badge reading "Lean" (this side is a lean). New code that shows a tier must go through `tierLabel`.

## Verify

npm install && npx playwright install chromium   # once

## Data pipeline

`scripts/update-data.js` is the "backend". `.github/workflows/track-prices.yml` runs `node scripts/update-data.js --track` **hourly for sixteen hours**, 10 AM to 1 AM Eastern (15:00-06:00 UTC): no Odds API requests, it refreshes DraftKings' open and current numbers from ESPN for every board pick and, for games starting within `sgoTrackWindowHours` (8), every book's price from SportsGameOdds, re-reads who plays for every posted NBA pick (the official injury report and ESPN's NBA list, two requests; see NBA availability under step 3), rebuilds the breakdowns, **grades every game that has finished** (free scores only, see step 5; it then rewrites `data/performance.js`, and leaves it alone when nothing was graded), rewrites `data/picks.js` (keeping `generatedAt`, adding `trackedAt`) and commits when the data, the log or the feeds changed (`git status --porcelain` over `data/picks.js`, `data/picks-log.json`, `data/performance.js`, `picks.ics`, `feed.xml`; `trackedAt` alone changes every run, so in practice it commits hourly). Results therefore land within the hour of a final, not at the next full run (owner complaint, 2026-11-05: picks "pop and go"). Cost: ESPN is free and unmetered; SportsGameOdds spends one entity per board game per hourly run inside the 8-hour window (roughly 6 games × 8 runs = 48 a day on a busy board, about 1,450 a month, plus the full runs and closes), with `sgoReserve` as the backstop. The full odds run cannot go hourly on The Odds API's free tier (3 credits per sport per run; two runs a day is the ceiling, and only for some sports: see the request budget). `.github/workflows/update-data.yml` runs the full pipeline twice a day, 14:00 UTC (9 AM Eastern in winter, 10 AM in summer; it was 17:00 UTC until 2026-11-05, when the owner moved it earlier because the replay audit found the model's information is worth more against prices nearer the open) and 20:30 UTC (late afternoon, before the evening slates), and on demand, then commits `data/` and redeploys. It passes `--slot morning|afternoon|manual` (a scheduled run from its cron line, a dispatch from its `slot` input), which decides which sports the run fetches (step 1). **Who starts them (2026-11-05):** GitHub's schedule fires hours late on this repo (on 2026-11-04 the 17:00 run started at 22:15 UTC and the 20:30 one at 00:59), so two Claude routines (claude.ai/code/routines, "EdgeLine morning data run" and "EdgeLine afternoon data run") dispatch the workflow on time with `slot=morning` / `slot=afternoon` (`gh workflow run update-data.yml -f slot=…`). The schedule stays as the fallback: the workflow's `gate` job (`scripts/slot-due.js`, tested by `scripts/slot-test.js`) skips any run for a slot that a full run has already covered since that slot's time (picks.js `generatedAt`), so a late duplicate never spends the credits twice; a manual dispatch (`slot` manual, the default) always runs. The morning slot passes `--no-lines` to the props step whichever way it was started. A 4 AM run found no edges; the two-run schedule is an experiment, and each logged pick records `loggedAt` so the log shows which run produces edges. Drop the weaker one later. It needs the `ODDS_API_KEY` repository secret (a key from https://the-odds-api.com; the free tier is 500 requests/month).

How a pick is made (all in `update-data.js`, tunable in `RULES`):
1. **Which sports a run fetches (2026-11-05; the owner: "we still need to do nfl and mlb and mls").** Sports: NFL, NBA, MLB, NHL, the Premier League and MLS (`SPORTS`; La Liga is switched off to stay inside the free tier). For each, fetch moneyline, spread and total prices (soccer: the three-way moneyline and the total) for ten named bookmakers: Pinnacle plus nine bettable U.S. books (ten bookmakers cost the same as one region: 3 credits per sport, 2 for soccer), only when there is a game to price, cheapest check first: (a) The Odds API's `/sports` list (free) leaves out a sport out of season; (b) the **slot rule** (`slots`, `slotAllows`): NFL, the Premier League, MLS and, since 2026-11-08, the NBA are fetched on the afternoon run only (the 36-hour window still reaches the next day: Friday's covers Saturday's soccer, Saturday's Sunday's NFL; the NBA because its afternoon read of the official injury report beat the morning one and the morning price had already moved most of the way to the close: see NBA availability under step 3 and The model against the market), NHL and MLB on both, and a manual run fetches everything; (c) **ESPN's scoreboard** (`espnGames`, free, read through the context source so the model's later read is cached) for every Eastern day the 36-hour window touches: a sport with no game starting inside the window is not asked at all, which covers NFL Thursdays, Saturdays and holidays and stops off-season spending; only when a day cannot be read and no game was found does the sport's old `days` rule decide (NFL Wed/Thu/Sat/Sun/Mon, Premier League Fri-Sun, MLS Tue/Wed/Fri-Sun; NBA/NHL/MLB every day); (d) the **morning guard** (`fetchDecision`): a morning run spends only what the month can spare after `RULES.afternoonReserve` (10) credits for every afternoon run left before the credits reset on the 1st (The Odds API's FAQ), plus the 25-credit floor, so when the month runs short the morning runs give way first; (e) the odds request itself asks only for games starting inside the window (`commenceTimeFrom` / `commenceTimeTo`, `oddsParams`), and per The Odds API's docs a request that returns no events costs nothing (should the API refuse the window, the request is repeated without it). Every full run prints one line for the Actions log (`planLine`): what it fetched, what each fetch cost (the `x-requests-last` header), what it skipped and why, and the credits left and used.
2. Per event and market, group books by exact line and keep the largest group (at least 3 books). The books' own number (`marketFair`, a reference that takes no part in the grade since 2026-11-04) is Pinnacle's de-vigged price for that line (the sharp reference). If Pinnacle posts a neighbouring line instead (NFL and NBA spreads and totals only, at most `maxAnchorShift` = 1 point away), its de-vigged price is **carried** to the books' line at a per-sport slope (`LINE_VALUE`: NFL 2.2%/pt spreads, 1.5%/pt totals; NBA 2.8%/pt and 1.4%/pt), the two sides renormalised to one; the pick records `anchorShift` and an `anchorNote`, the rationale and breakdown say so, and the breakdown files it under notes rather than support ("a fair estimate, not a quote"). Hockey, baseball and soccer lines move in steps too big to convert. Only when neither applies does the de-vigged average of the other books stand in. Until 2026-11-04 this number was the grade and the site called it "Fair"; it now lives in `marketFair` / `marketEdge` and is shown as "where the market sits".
3. **The game model grades every side (owner rule, 2026-11-04: plays and props are graded on game data, never against other books' prices).** Before pricing, `model.forEvent()` finds the game on ESPN's scoreboard for its Eastern date, reads both teams' schedules (completed regular-season games before tip-off: points for and against, margins, the last five; soccer marks league matches with the season's own id rather than type 2, which until 2026-11-04 made the filter drop every Premier League game, so no EPL game had a model), the league injury report and rest (`restFromSchedule`: calendar days in Eastern time, so a game the night before is a back-to-back whatever the tip-off hours say). `gameModel()` turns that into an expected margin (home minus away) and total: each team's rating is (1 − form) × season margin + form × last-five margin (form 0.1 in the NBA, NFL, NHL, EPL and MLS, 0 in MLB), shrunk toward zero by gp / (gp + k) (k: NBA 6, NFL 6, EPL 8, MLS 20, NHL 35, MLB 60). **Carry-over:** where the club has last season in the league (`lastSeasonParam` names the season as ESPN numbers it: NBA and NHL by the year a season ends, NFL and EPL by the year it starts, MLB and MLS by their year; `scheduleSeason` fetches it, one free request a club a run), the rating starts from part of last season’s margin per game instead of zero: (gp × raw + carry.k × carry.c × last) / (gp + carry.k), with carry NBA 60% over 10 games, NFL 40% over 6, NHL 25% over 40, MLB 50% over 80, EPL 75% over 15, MLS 25% over 20 (fitted 2026-11-05 on held-out seasons, results only; early-season error fell about 5% in the NBA and EPL, 3% in the NFL); a promoted club keeps the old k and starts from zero; plus the sport's home edge (NBA 1.9, NFL 2.3, NHL 0.24, MLB 0.03, EPL 0.24, MLS 0.5), a back-to-back penalty and a 3+ days' rest bonus (NBA −1.5 / +0.5, NHL −0.25 / +0.05, EPL rest only), and injuries in the sport's points (an NBA player listed Out costs 12% of his points a game from `data/faces.js`, 1.2 when unknown; an NFL quarterback 4; a goalie 0.5; a starting pitcher 0.3; day-to-day or questionable counts half, and an NBA listing from the league's official report counts at how often its status sits (see NBA availability below); a goalie or a quarterback counts once per team, the costliest listing, because only one plays: `SPORT[].single`, fixed 2026-11-04 after two Rangers goalies listed Out stacked to a full goal); the total is the four scoring averages halved, each first pulled toward the league's scoring per team-game this season by `kTotal` games (NFL 5, MLS 15, MLB 20, EPL 30, NHL 80; the NBA keeps raw averages), read once a run from ESPN's standings (`leagueGoalsFrom`: points for over games played, or over wins + losses + ties where the table has no games column), `leagueGoals` as the fallback. Injuries take nothing off a total except an NFL quarterback out (2 points): a league's average already carries its usual injury load, and the old per-player deductions pulled every total under (on 2026-11-05's slates the NFL's projected totals averaged 35.4 against the league's 46.1, the NBA's 5 points low, the NHL's 0.4 goals low). **Injuries count only for the share of the team's results that still had the player, which is the games he played** (since 2026-11-05): `forEvent` reads each costed listing's ESPN game log (`site.web.api.espn.com/apis/common/v3/sports/<sport>/<league>/athletes/<id>/gamelog`, free, one request a player a run, cached by player and spaced like the other ESPN calls; about 6 a game, 146 for 22 NBA and NHL games on 2026-11-05) and sets `played`: his appearances among the games the team's rating counts (regular season, completed, before tip-off), matched by event id and by the club he played for, so a preseason game, a cup tie or a game for his old club never counts (`playedIn`). `unseenShare` is then played over the team's games: a player who has missed every game is already in every result and costs nothing more, one who played them all costs in full, half the games half. An MLB pitcher appears by turn (a healthy starter in one game in five), so his count is the stretch of the team's games from his first appearance to his last. The log is asked for without a season, so ESPN returns the player's latest season and names it: an older one means no games this season (`appearances` in `context.js`; with `?season=` the NHL answers a player with no games with a 404). ESPN's injury report carries no `athlete.id` in any league, so `teamInjuries` reads it from the player-card link or the headshot (`athleteIdOf`; until 2026-11-05 every `athleteId` on the site was null). A listing that costs nothing anyway is not looked up; one with no id or no readable log falls back to the listing date (games before it over games played). Only the players who still move the number (a share of a fifth or more) are named in the reasons, so a player out all season is not named. Why: ESPN re-dates its entries every few days, so the listing date charged players who had not played at all. On 2026-11-05, 41 of the 91 NBA players on the report had not played a game this season and the listing date still charged 22 of them half or more (Porzingis, 0 of 8 and listed the day before, in full); on that day's 11 NBA games the injury cost fell from 42.1 to 18.2 points and margins moved about a point (2.6 at most, Thunder at Grizzlies), and on 11 NHL games from 6.1 to 2.9 goals (Korpisalo and Montembeault, goalies with no game this season, had each been charged as the starter). `sideProb()` reads a league's own distribution of final margins and totals, tilted to the game's projected margin and total, wherever the calibration chose it: the NHL (`NHL_SHAPE`, both markets: no ties, overtime's extra goal, a two-goal lead becoming three with the goalie pulled), MLB (`MLB_SHAPE`, both: one-run finals are 28%, and the margins are kept from the home side's view because the home team does not bat in the ninth when ahead, so home teams win 53% while out-scoring visitors by 0.03 runs), the EPL (`EPL_SHAPE`, both, with the draw: a quarter of matches), MLS (`MLS_SHAPE`, both, with the draw; its margins kept from the home side's view, like MLB's, because MLS's home edge is large) and the NFL (`NFL_SHAPE`, margins only: finals pile on 3 and 7); everywhere else (NBA margins and totals, NFL totals) the normal CDF at the sport's spread of outcomes (`SPORT[].sd` / `sdTotal`: NBA 14.3 / 19, NFL 13.3 / 13.4, NHL 2.4 / 2.3, MLB 4.5 / 4.5, EPL 1.7 / 1.6, MLS 1.63 / 1.71): moneyline, spread (push-aware on whole numbers), total, and the draw as the band of ±0.5 goals around the margin for soccer. **Then the league's calibration for that market** (`CALIB` in `model.js`, 2026-11-05; see The model against the market below): p' = 1 / (1 + e^−(a + b·logit p)) on the home or over side, the other side its complement, fitted on held-out seasons from results only (each side's probability read at the line the game was played at; no price takes part). NBA spreads and totals (b 0.05 and 0.04) and NFL spreads and totals (0.08 and 0) read about even, because at the books' lines our numbers did worse than a coin flip on every held-out season; the NBA moneyline is stretched (a −0.03, b 1.17: it was too flat); the NHL, MLB, EPL and MLS, and the NFL moneyline, are left raw (no map beat the raw numbers held out; MLB and MLS untested). `rawSideProb` is the model alone; `sideProb` (what every caller reads, `noise()` included, so a calibrated side's bar is in calibrated points) applies the map; a side the map pulls toward even says so in its reasons ("Past seasons: our totals at the books' lines did no better than a coin flip, so this side reads 50.8% (the model alone 69.6%)"). A market with no signal (`model.hasSignal`: a calibration slope under 0.5, so NBA and NFL spreads and totals) is never a pick or a watch, whatever the price: its sides read about even, and a plus-money price would otherwise put a coin flip past the 3-point floor; it stays on the market board as information. `priceWithModel()` writes that probability into `fair` / `modelProb` for every priced side and the edge as our probability − implied(best bettable price, never Pinnacle); a game with no ESPN record (no scoreboard match, no completed games) has no probability on any side and can never be a pick (`modelled` false on its market rows). **The bar (Phase 2, 2026-11-05; the owner said the board "feels favored to sports" and approved "fewer, steadier picks"): every side has its own.** `model.noise()` is the model's own error on that side from the size of the sample alone: the standard error of the rating difference behind the margin, sd × √(1/(gpHome + kHome) + 1/(gpAway + kAway)) with k the prior's weight in games (`carry.k` where the club has last season, else the shrinkage `k`), or for a total sdTotal / 2 × √(1/(gpHome + kTotal) + 1/(gpAway + kTotal)), moved through the side's own probability at its line one error each way (the mean move, so a draw still gets one). The bar is `RULES.barZ` (1) of those errors and never under `RULES.minEdge` (3 points, the floor): early in a season about 9 to 12 points on an NBA side, 6 to 7 on an NHL one, 7 to 15 in the NFL, 3 on most NHL totals, falling toward 3 as the games pile up. Checked against held-out seasons (results only): the formula's early-season margin error, NBA 4.9 / NHL 0.50 / MLB 0.68 / EPL 0.51, against a measured 5.4 / 0.52 / 0.63 / 0.60 (NFL 5.2 against 3.3, cautious; MLS 0.45 against 0.51, 2025). A flat 3-point bar sat inside that noise, so the noisiest sport filled the board: on the 11-04 board it took 9 picks on 8 games, all High; the per-side bar took 8 (NHL 4, NBA 3, NFL 1), mostly Medium. The tier is how far the edge clears the side's own bar (`past` = edge − bar): Lean past it, Medium 2 points past (`RULES.medium`), High 5 (`RULES.high`); a thin sample (under four games behind either team, `model.thin`) is capped at Lean. A pick logged before 2026-11-05 has no `bar` and was graded on the flat 3 / 5 / 8. Each pick records `bar`, `past` and `barParts` { error, units, unit, gp, floor }; the rationale, factors and breakdown give the bar in words ("Its bar is 9.2 points: the model's own margin of error on this side, with 8 games behind the thinner team (±4.8 points on the margin). It clears it by 3.1."). Each pick records `basis: "game"`, `model` (margin, total, sd, gp, thin, `parts`, and `reasons` for and against the side), `marketFair` / `marketEdge`, `ev` (expected profit per unit) and a rationale that names the book, states the rule and gives the market number as a reference. Only one side of a market can be picked. The price-edge grade is gone from the pipeline; picks logged before the change have no `basis` and read as "price" in the record. Every league was calibrated on 2026-11-04 from ESPN final scores only, never prices: three full seasons each for the NBA (3,693 games), NFL (816), MLB (7,288) and EPL (1,102), two for the NHL (2,624), fit on one season and tested on the others. **MLS** joined on 2026-11-05 the same way (results only): fitted on the 2023 and 2024 regular seasons (986 matches; home sides won 45%, drew 26% and out-scored visitors by 0.47 goals a game), tested on 2025 (495 matches with a game behind both clubs) and on 2026 to date (480). It is a parity league: the fit kept only a quarter of last season over 20 games of evidence. Held out on 2025, the margin's error was 1.700 goals against 1.748 for the home edge alone (2.7% lower; 3.4% on 2026), the 1X2 log loss 1.0448 against 1.0760 for the base rates (Brier 0.628 against 0.651), draws predicted at 24.2% against 24.8%; totals barely beat the league average (over/under 2.5 log loss 0.680 against 0.682, 3.5 0.637 against 0.641). Its bars are wide: about 7 to 8 points on a moneyline side with 33 games behind each club, the 3-point floor on most draws. ESPN's MLS injury report is empty, so the injury cost rarely applies, and rest is not used. Recent form earned a weight of 0 to 0.1 everywhere (it was 0.4); in MLB and the NHL the old settings predicted margins worse than no rating at all; the NBA's curve was too narrow (12.5 where the misses spread 14.3). The injury costs on the margin cannot be tested that way (ESPN keeps no injury history); how much of a team's results already lack a player is read from his game log, not from the listing date ESPN keeps re-dating. Otherwise the model is uncalibrated on day one: its numbers are deterministic and come only from the game data, and the first weeks of `byBasis` results and CLV are how to judge the per-sport spreads and costs in `SPORT`.
   **NBA availability (2026-11-08; the owner chose "NBA lineups and late injury news": the model should know who actually plays tonight).** `scripts/availability.js` reads the league's official injury report, a PDF the NBA publishes every 15 minutes, all day, at `ak-static.cms.nba.com/referee/injury/Injury-Report_YYYY-MM-DD_HH_MMAM|PM.pdf` (Eastern time; hourly `_05PM` names, stamped at :30, until mid-2025-26; every report since 2023-24 is still there). Clubs list every player who may miss the game as Out, Doubtful, Questionable, Probable or Available, with the reason, by 5 PM local the day before (back-to-backs by 1 PM ET on game day); from 2023-24 to 2026-11-04, 17.6% of clubs had not yet submitted at the morning run, 2.3% at the afternoon run, 0.3% at 5:30 PM. It is parsed with no dependency (`parseReport`: FlateDecode content streams and WinAnsi fonts; each table cell is a clip rectangle holding its words, spaced by the font's widths; the column heads are on page one only, blank cells fill down, "NOT YET SUBMITTED" marks a club). Once a run (`model.availabilityFor`; the context source's `officialReport`: the newest PDF at or before the run, 15 minutes back at a time, a normal browser user agent) `forEvent` merges it with ESPN's list per club and game, newest first (`mergeTeam`): the report's status replaces ESPN's (one player by his whole name or his last name and first initial, "Nic" / "Nicolas Claxton"; never the last name alone, which on a roster paired a two-way Moses Brown with Bruce Brown); a player only the report names gets his ESPN id from the club's roster (`teams/<id>/roster`, one free request a club, only then), and one no roster places costs nothing; a player ESPN lists but a newer submitted report leaves off is Available; an ESPN entry newer than the report wins; a club the report does not carry or has not submitted for keeps ESPN's list. Each report listing carries `miss`, how often that status sat (`MISS`: every report at the morning run, the afternoon run and 5:30 PM from 2023-24 to 2026-11-04, 1,512 PDFs, against ESPN's box scores, weighted by scoring: Out 99%, Doubtful 96 to 99%, Questionable 41 to 43%, Probable 4 to 7%, Available 3 to 5%, against 2.9% for a rotation player no report listed; set to 1 / 0.97 / 0.42 / 0.04 / 0), and the cost is the sport's cost × `miss` × the played share; under `model.MIN_MISS` (0.1: Probable, Available) nothing, and the player is not named. The reasons name the report ("Milwaukee Bucks without Damian Lillard (G) (+1.2), out per the 9:30 AM ET official report", "Boston Celtics have Sam Hauser (F) questionable per the 9:30 AM ET official report", "Philadelphia 76ers have Joel Embiid (C) probable per the 10:15 AM ET official report (ESPN still lists him day-to-day)"). `gm.availability` { read, report { source, label, at, url }, onReport { home, away }, home / away: [{ name, pos, athleteId, status, miss, source ("official" or "espn"), at, report, reason, played, cost, full }] } is logged with every NBA pick as `availability.posted` (`full`: his cost were he out). `RULES.officialReport` false reads ESPN's list alone, exactly as before. **Holds** (`RULES.availabilityHold`, `availability.unresolvedOf`): on the afternoon and manual runs an NBA game whose clubs are not both on the report yet (a later day's game: the 36-hour window would otherwise pick tomorrow's games on the day before's news) posts nothing, so every NBA pick is made on its own game day's report; the level for a morning run ("open") also holds a game with a costed player still questionable, should the NBA return to it. A held game is named in the Actions log ("NBA games held until who plays is in") and its market rows carry `waiting` (why). An unreadable report holds nothing. **After posting** every full run (from the re-run model) and every hourly track run (the report and ESPN's NBA list; a newly listed player priced from `data/faces.js`, 12% of his points) compares the read with what the pick was posted on (`changesSince`) and flags a status that moved the game's margin by `RULES.availabilityFlag` (0.5) points or more: `availability` { changed, note ("Paul George ruled out at 5:30 PM ET (official report), after this pick was posted at 10:00 AM ET; about 2.5 points of margin against this side on our numbers"), swing (points toward the pick; null for a total, which the model does not move for injuries), changes [{ side, name, from, to, fromMiss, toMiss, delta, at (when first read), source, report }], checkedAt, read }. It is only a flag: the pick stays posted, locked and graded at its posted price; the breakdown lists the note as a risk (with that line), as support, or for a total as a note. A read without the report (unreadable) leaves the last flag standing; one that matches what the pick was posted on clears it. **Sources** (checked 2026-11-08): the official report (used); ESPN's injuries endpoint (already used; its game summaries show today's injuries even for past games, so no history); the NBA's own daily lineups file (`stats.nba.com/js/data/leaders/00_daily_lineups_YYYYMMDD.json`: expected and confirmed starters with timestamps, same terms as the report; not wired: the cost is by absence, which the report already lists, and its archive keeps only each day's final state, so it cannot be replayed); projected-lineup sites rejected: RotoWire sells its lineup and injury data through a licensed API and sued a company for scraping it; ESPN keeps no intraday odds (`odds/<provider>/history/0/movement` is empty), so the timing evidence uses the committed market files. **Terms**: NBA.com's terms of use (updated 2026-07-13) have no clause against automated access and robots.txt allows `/referee/` (Crawl-Delay 1; one request a run); they keep the materials for personal, non-commercial use and ask written permission for public or commercial reuse. Like ESPN's data: fine for this demo, a licensed feed (Sportradar, SportsDataIO) or the league's permission before the service is sold. Only facts (a player's status at a time) are used, never the document.
4. **One pick per game, every sport on one scale** (`analyzeEvent` + `selectPicks`, Phase 2): each game offers at most its one main-line side furthest past its own bar, across moneyline, spread and total (a moneyline and a spread on the same team are one bet); every game's candidate from every sport is ranked by points past its bar, and a game that already has a pick (from this run or an earlier one, any status but void) gets no second; at most `RULES.maxPerDay` (10) picks per Eastern game day and `RULES.maxPerSportDay` (5) per sport that day, counting what earlier runs posted. Only events starting within 36 hours. `RULES.onePerGame: false` restores the old one-per-market rule (the offline tests use it, with `barZ: 0`, for the fixture's five-pick checks). A pick is logged once per event+market+outcome+line, at the price seen, and **is locked** (owner decision, 2026-11-05): it is never withdrawn, whatever the price or the model does later, and is graded at the posted price. A posted NBA pick carries `availability` (what the model knew about who plays when it was posted, and whether that has changed: see NBA availability under step 3); a change only flags it. On every later run before tip-off, `priceOutcomes()` prices **every** outcome on every line two or more books share (not just the main line, not just positive edges) so each pending pick's `current` price and `history` keep updating even after its edge is gone or its line is no longer the main one (`current.fair` and `history[].fair` carry the market's number, for the close; `current.modelProb`, `current.edge` and `current.bar` are ours, re-run on the latest data). That is what makes the close real.
5. Pending picks whose games have started are graded (`gradeStarted()`, shared by full and hourly runs) as soon as a source says the game is final: ESPN's scoreboard with its completed flag grades at once; a source with no completed flag is trusted only 3 hours after the start (`RULES.gradeAfterHours`); a game ESPN shows still in progress waits. `grade()` reads the market from `marketKey` (until 2026-11-05 it read the display label in `market`, so every moneyline and spread would have been graded as a total: the Flyers ML winning 5-2 came out a loss; a regression check guards it). Hourly runs never call The Odds API and never void anything. Scores come from free sources first: ESPN's scoreboard for the game's ET date (already fetched for context), then SportsGameOdds' results; **The Odds API scores endpoint is only called, in a full run, for a sport with picks neither could settle 3+ hours after the start**, which cuts its credit use by roughly a third. Win pays the profit at the logged odds for 1 unit; loss is −1; push 0. Ungraded after 7 days becomes `void` and is excluded. At grading, `close` and `clv` are written. The close comes first from **SportsGameOdds** (`close.source` = "consensus close (SportsGameOdds, N books)"): the vendor's de-vigged fair at its closing line when that line is ours (always for a moneyline), else our own de-vig across the books still on our line; then from **ESPN's core odds record** for the game (`sports.core.api.espn.com/.../odds/100`, DraftKings, free, no key): after a game it holds `open`, `current` and `close` per side, so every matched pick gets a true closing number regardless of when we last ran (`close.source` = "DraftKings close (ESPN)"); failing that, the last Odds API history point before tip-off ("last check (Odds API)"); failing both, null. **CLV** = closing fair probability (DraftKings' two-way price de-vigged, or our fair at the last check) − implied probability of the logged price, in probability points; positive means the price we took beat the close. CLV is a market measure: evidence that the price was good, never part of the grade. For spreads and totals the price CLV is only computed when the close is on our number; otherwise `clv` is null and `lineMove` says whether the line moved `toward` or `away` from the pick (spread: a worse number for our side is toward; total: up for an over, down for an under). Draws have no ESPN close (two-way block only). **Soccer settles on 90 minutes** (2026-11-05, for MLS's playoffs): the 1X2 and the goals total are graded on the score after 90 minutes and stoppage time. ESPN's score after extra time includes it (`STATUS_FINAL_AET`, or `STATUS_FINAL_PEN` after a shootout that followed extra time), so `context.regulationScore` rebuilds it from the goal list (scoring plays that are not shootout kicks, at or before 5,400 seconds) and returns null, so the game waits, when the list does not add up to the final; SportsGameOdds' result is read from its `reg` period for soccer. The Odds API's scores (the last resort) carry no such split.
6. `performance.js` summarises graded picks from the last 30 days by event date (Eastern), plus `byDate` (all-time daily profit for the calendar), `potd` and `parlays` records, and `clv` (`n` picks with price CLV, `avg` in points, `beat`, `beatPct`, `graded`, `lineToward`, `lineAway`, `espnCloses`), and `byBasis` (for `game` picks and for the earlier `price` picks: wins, losses, pushes, units, `n`, `roi`, `pending` and `since`, kept apart so the old grade never flatters or drags the new one); each `recent` row carries `basis`, `clv`, `closeOdds`, `closeLine`, `closeSource` and `lineMove`. `picks.js` is the board first (every pending pick whose event is still ahead, highest edge first) and then **every single pick whose game started within the last `RULES.cardHours` (18) hours, whatever its status**, earliest first, so a started pick stays on the day's card through its live game and result instead of vanishing (the pages split them by the clock; see Line shopping, live status, digest). Every pick carries `status` (the log's pending / graded / void), `started` (at the time the file was written), `result` ("Win" / "Loss" / "Push" once graded), `units` (profit for one unit once graded), `score` ({ home, away } final, from the grading source), `loggedAt` (when it was posted) `valueTo` (the worst whole American price at which our probability still clears the pick's own bar: implied ≤ modelProb − bar, the 3-point floor for a pick without one; null when that is not between 0 and 1), `bar`, `past`, `barParts`, `now` ({ at, odds, book, modelProb, edge, bar } from the latest full run that re-priced it: the pending pick's `current`; null until a later run has) and `standing` ("clears" its bar now, "below" it but still positive, "gone" under zero; null without a later check). The pick stays on the card whatever its standing; the page says whether the edge still holds. Internal choices (play and parlay of the day, the SportsGameOdds and DraftKings refreshes, the breakdowns) still look only at upcoming pending picks. A game from the previous Eastern day is labelled "Yesterday 8:00 PM ET". Each pick also has `prices` (price at every bettable book on that line), `books`, `anchor`, `ev`, `outcome`, `commence` and `sportKey` (the digest orders by `commence`; live status needs `sportKey`, `commence`, `outcome` and `context.espnEventId`). An NBA pick logged since 2026-11-08 also has `availability` { changed, note, swing, changes, checkedAt, posted { read, report { source, label, at, url }, players: [{ side, name, status, source, at }] } } (who it was posted knowing about, and any change since; null for other leagues and older picks).
7. **Play of the day** (`playOfDay` in picks.js): the highest-EV pick on the board, chosen once per Eastern day at the first run that has picks (`potd` date on the log entry) and never swapped that day. **Parlay of the day** (`parlayOfDay`): 2-3 legs from different games, all +EV at one book, the combination with the highest EV (`fairProb` = the product of the legs’ probabilities from the game model, treating legs as independent); logged as a `type: "parlay"` entry with `legPrices`, one per day. Parlays grade once every leg settles: any loss loses, pushes drop out, the rest pay the combined price; a void leg voids it.
8. **Breakdowns.** After the board is built, `scripts/context.js` enriches every pending pick (free sources, each optional): ESPN injuries for both teams (status, type, return date, note), the ESPN scoreboard for that date (venue, indoor flag, records, probable pitchers, TV, ESPN's own weather, and the DraftKings odds block: open and current spread, total and moneyline, stored as `context.odds`; the pick's own side is summarised in `dk` {open, now, moved, label} and appended to `dkHistory` whenever it changes, up to 48 points), each team's schedule (rest days, back-to-back, games in the last 7 days), and Open-Meteo wind/rain/temperature at kickoff for outdoor games (city geocoded from the venue). The pipeline also records every price it sees for a logged pick (`current`, `history`) so a breakdown can show line movement. `buildBreakdown()` writes: the tier and exactly why (our probability, what the price implies, the edge against the side's own bar and why the bar is what it is, the Medium and High steps past it, and the sentence that nothing is graded against other books' prices), the rationale, "what supports it" and "what could go wrong" (the model's reasons for and against the side: ratings and records, form, home edge, rest, injuries with their cost in points, the projected margin or total against the number; for a modelled side the context's own injury and rest lines stand down so nothing is listed twice, while totals keep "scorers out" and "tired legs"; without a model the old rules of thumb still apply), notes and a one-line verdict. Rules of thumb that remain for every pick: wind 15+ mph, rain 50%+ or freezing lean under; an edge under its bar at current prices is a risk (worded so it is clear the pick stays posted and is graded at the posted price); a better price at another book is support. Every market line is a note prefixed "Market:" (where the market sits, the sharp line's move since the pick was logged, DraftKings' open → now, the nine-book consensus since the open) and never support or risk; the dialog's Line movement cell keeps the ▲/▼ tag as information. "Benching" and rest decisions surface through the injury report's status/type (e.g. "Out, Rest"); in the NBA the league's official report is the game-day source (see NBA availability under step 3); there is no projected-lineup feed (none free whose terms allow it). A posted NBA pick whose availability changed after it was posted gets the change's note: a risk when it hurts the side (with the locked-pick line), support when it helps, a note for a total. The site shows all of this in the Full breakdown dialog (`EdgeLine.openBreakdown`), reachable from every card back, the play of the day and each parlay's legs.
9. The picks page lists the best parlays client-side: `EdgeLine.bestParlays` enumerates every 2-4 leg combination of distinct games that is +EV at a single book, keeps the best book per leg set, and ranks by EV. The parlay builder re-uses `prices` the same way: `EdgeLine.parlayMath` prices a slip at one book (auto-best, chosen, or mixed best prices with a warning). The slip lives in localStorage (`parlay-slip`, `parlay-book`).

## The model against the market (held-out check, 2026-11-05)

The owner asked why the board is "just one sport or heavily favorited" (11 NHL games: 66 sides, 32 with a positive edge, 5 picks, four of them underdogs on +1.5 at -130 to -190). The model was replayed game by game over past seasons with only what was known before each game (no injuries: ESPN keeps no history), and compared with ESPN's free core odds (`sports.core.api.espn.com/v2/sports/<sport>/leagues/<league>/events/<id>/competitions/<id>/odds`: ESPN BET or DraftKings open and close for every regular-season game replayed: NHL 2024-25 on, NBA 2023-24 on, NFL 2024 on, EPL where ESPN has them) **for evaluation only**. The scripts and cached data are in the session scratchpad (`calib/`), not the repo. Findings:

- **The model knows less than the market, so its disagreements point to underdogs.** It is calibrated against results (NHL moneyline slope 1.10 ± 0.13, NBA 1.17 ± 0.05, NFL 1.03 ± 0.12; 1 is perfect), but flatter than the market: on the closing favourite's side the NHL model averaged 56.0% where the market said 59.2% and favourites won 58.8% (NBA 64.0 / 69.0 / 69.6; NFL 60.6 / 66.4 / 67.2). Regressed on both, results carry no weight on the model beyond the closing price (NHL moneyline −0.10 ± 0.22, puck line 0.11 ± 0.17, totals −0.10 ± 0.25; NBA moneyline 0.01 ± 0.10), so every edge against the close is the model's missing information plus the vig. Picks made by today's rule, priced at the close: NHL 883 over 2.2 seasons, 87% underdogs, ROI −4.8% ± 3.7; NBA 2,494, 84% underdogs, −7.9% ± 2.3; NFL 253, −9.5% ± 7.1. Against the **opening** line the model does carry information (NHL puck line 0.43 ± 0.19, NBA moneyline 0.25 ± 0.10) and the same picks returned NHL +6.4% ± 4.9, NBA −1.8% ± 2.5: the market prices it in by the close, so the earlier run (morning, not afternoon) is the better one to keep.
- **No NHL setting fixes it.** Shrinkage and carry-over hardly move held-out log-loss (0.6747 production and 0.6747 at the best of 84 settings; the favourite's side stays at 56 to 57%); a two-season prior did no better. `NHL_SHAPE` is right: put at the margin the closing moneyline implies, it gives the market's own puck line within 0.1 to 0.4 points and the covers within their error (home −1.5: 38.3% by the shape, 38.3% by the market, 37.5% covered). The first week is the one exception (a team's first 7 games: slope 2.5 ± 0.6, the ratings too flat).
- **Spreads and totals at the books' lines: no signal in the NBA and NFL.** The NBA model's margin sits 3.7 points from the closing spread (sd) and its total 6.1 from the closing total, and the result moved 0.0 points per point of either gap. That is what `CALIB` corrects. Mid-season those sides would have been most of the board (NBA picks a game rise from 0.25 at 5 to 8 games to 0.80 after 30 games as the bars fall from 12.8 to 6.3 points; 1,728 of the replay's 2,494 NBA picks were totals).
- **Why one sport on 2026-11-05:** the calendar and the overnight run. NBA moneyline bars were 12.8 points at 5 to 8 games against the NHL's 7.5 at 13 to 16, and four of the five NHL picks were posted at 05:10 UTC, before the per-side bar, carry-over and the played-share injury fix (the Rangers were charged 1.4 goals for two goalies and three skaters, the Oilers 1.7); re-priced by today's model none of the four clears its bar (Flyers +1.5: 66.7% against −190, edge 1.2 against a 6.4 bar). They stay posted (locked).
- **Open for the owner:** whether to keep posting picks the replay cannot show beat the close (a higher `barZ` only thins them: at 1.5 the NHL makes 0.16 a game, still −4.9% at the close; the NBA's and NFL's moneyline picks get worse as the bar rises, the sign of information the model lacks); whether likeliest-first should stay the default when the likeliest sides are underdogs on +1.5 at −190; and data the model lacks (NHL starting goalies, NBA lineups) as the only route to a real edge.

- **NBA game-day availability (held-out check, 2026-11-08).** The NBA keeps every official injury report since 2023-24, so the replay above was run again with what each report said at the morning run (14:00 UTC), the afternoon run (20:30 UTC) and 5:30 PM ET, its listed players costed through the production model (12% of their points a game over their share of the club's games, from ESPN's box scores, at `MISS` by status), against no injuries (the audit above) and against who actually sat (the box scores). 3,728 games from 2023-24 to 2026-11-04, each season held out in turn (calibration refitted on the others; scripts in the session scratchpad's `jobs/avail-*.js`, not the repo). Moneyline log-loss: none 0.6011, morning 0.5955, afternoon 0.5901, 5:30 PM 0.5897, who actually sat 0.5894; the market 0.5883 at the open, 0.5768 at the close. Paired, game by game (×10⁻⁴): morning −56 ± 21 against none, afternoon −109 ± 21, 5:30 PM −114 ± 22; afternoon against morning −53 ± 16. The model's weight beyond the market rose at the open from 0.22 ± 0.09 to 0.46 ± 0.09 (afternoon read) and stayed nil at the close (0.01 ± 0.08 to 0.08 ± 0.10): the market prices the news by tip-off. The pick rule (moneyline only, one a game past its own bar) priced at the open: none −3.3% ± 5.4 (828 picks), morning +6.1% ± 5.7 (764), afternoon +11.8% ± 5.5 (752), 5:30 PM +14.4% ± 5.4 (772); at the close −8.0% ± 5.0 (953), −9.2% ± 5.1 (831), −4.8% ± 5.7 (671), −5.1% ± 5.8 (660). NBA spreads still show no signal at the closing line (slope 0.06 ± 0.10 with the afternoon read), so `CALIB` stays. The old status rule on the same 5:30 PM report (out and doubtful in full, questionable and probable at half) scored 0.5907 against 0.5897 with `MISS`. **Timing:** priced part of the way from the open to the close, the morning read returns +6.1% at the open, −1.0% a quarter of the way, −5.5% halfway, −7.6% three quarters; the afternoon read +11.8%, +7.7%, +1.0%, −3.8% and −4.2% at 90%. The committed market files since 2026-11-04 (18 NBA games, 42 snapshots of Pinnacle's price; ESPN keeps no intraday odds) put the price 3 to 5 hours before tip-off (the afternoon run) at 92% of the way from DraftKings' open to its close and 8 to 12 hours before (the morning run) at about 84% (5 snapshots): the morning price is not the open. A third of the morning picks (242 to 286 of about 735) had the afternoon report move the margin a point or more against them, locked; 17.6% of clubs had not submitted at the morning run. So the NBA moved to the afternoon run only (`slots`, 3 credits a day saved) with games held until both clubs are on the report (`RULES.availabilityHold`). A morning run that waited only on unresolved games (76% of games at the morning run) came out between the two (−2.1% to −5.7% as the morning price moves from a quarter to three quarters of the way) and is kept as the "open" hold level. Neither run beats the close: the replay still cannot show a real edge in the NBA.

## The per-side bar on the page (client, 2026-11-05)

The pipeline gives every pick and market row its own bar (see Data pipeline). ## The market board and the never-empty rule

Every full run writes `data/market.js`: both sides of every main-line market it priced (`priceOutcomes` rows with `qualifies`), with event, start label, best price and book, every book's price, our probability (`modelProb`, null when the game has no model, with `modelled` false), edge (ours, null without a model), EV, `marketFair` / `marketEdge` (the books' number, reference only), `model` (margin, total, gp, thin), anchor and `anchorShift`, each with its own `bar` and `past` (null without a model), flagged `status`: `pick` (a pending pick on the board, with `pickId`), `watch` (edge of at least half its own bar, `RULES.watchShare`, and at least `RULES.watchEdge` = a point and a half; never a pick) or `none`. The file carries `basis: "game"`, `threshold` (the floor of every bar, 3 points), `watch`, `barRule` { floor, z, watchShare, medium, high }, `bars` (per sport: min, median and max bar across its modelled sides) and `modelled` (games with a model). Sorted by our edge, unmodelled sides last; `--track` runs leave it alone. A sport a full run does not fetch (its slot, the morning guard, the quota) keeps its rows from the last file for games still ahead (`carriedRows`), each with `pricedAt` (when its prices were read) and its pick / watch flag re-read from the log, so Sunday's morning run never empties the NFL from the market board and the Games page an hour before kickoff. The picks page shows it as the **Market board** (`#market`): summary line, sport / market / status / search filters, 40 rows at a time with a pick row linking to its card.

**Owner rule (2026-11-04): nothing on the site stays empty.** When no pick clears the bar, every slot falls back to the closest markets, always labelled Watch / No bet and never dressed as a play: the home ticker and hero board ("Closest to the bar"), the play-of-the-day slot (`potdCard(null)` shows the nearest miss with a note), the parlay slot and the best-parlays list (`pulseCard`: games, markets, watch count, top three), the top-three grid and the picks grid (market cards: status badge instead of a confidence badge, no parlay button, a Market board link instead of Full breakdown), and the digest (lede, tiles, top three, slate retitled "The watch list"). `EdgeLine.marketRows()` does the dressing (`marketAsPick`): a row counts as a pick only while its `pickId` is on the current board; otherwise it is market information whatever the file says. Keep this rule when adding slots.

## Subscriptions: the calendar and the feed (`scripts/feeds.js`)

Shipped 2026-11-04 as the session's bold change (the owner asked for one that improves conversions; not yet reviewed). The site had no way for a visitor to come back: every sign-up button was a dead `#`. The feeds are the zero-backend answer. `node scripts/feeds.js` reads `data/picks.js`, `data/market.js` and `data/picks-log.json` and writes two files at the repo root, deterministic for a given set of data files so the workflows only commit when a run changed something (both workflows run it after the pipeline and include the two files in the diff check and the commit):

- **`picks.ics`**: `VCALENDAR` with a one-hour refresh hint. One `VEVENT` per board pick, `UID` = `<pick id>@edgeline`, `DTSTART` = `commence` (UTC), three hours long, a `VALARM` 30 minutes before, `SUMMARY` = "Play of the day: " prefix when it is, then `pick odds at book · tier`, `DESCRIPTION` = the rationale, support, risks, verdict, the price ladder and the breakdown link, `LOCATION` = the venue when known. Every pick is written once from a merged record (the log entry when present, else picks.js), so a started pick on picks.js is not duplicated. Log entries from the last 30 days that are not on the board keep the **same UID**: a pick in play reads "· in play", a graded one "· Won +1.40u" / "· Lost −1.00u" / "· Push" with CLV in the description, so the calendar updates in place and becomes the public record. Void entries are left out. On a no-play night an all-day event (`slate-<ET date>@edgeline`) carries the never-empty line ("No play tonight: N sides priced in G games, none clears the bar") and the three closest sides, one per market. Lines are folded at 75 octets and text is escaped per RFC 5545 (`icsText`, `fold`).
- **`feed.xml`**: RSS 2.0 with an `atom:link rel="self"`. One item per board pick (`guid` = pick id, `pubDate` = `loggedAt`), one per parlay of the day, one per grade (`guid` = `<id>#graded`, a new item so readers notify), and one per full run (`guid` = `slate-<generatedAt>`, so hourly track runs do not add items). Newest first, slate first on a tie, 80 items at most. Descriptions are escaped HTML built from the same text lines (`html()`); never raw data.
- The site: `index.html#subscribe` (between the plays and how it works) shows the next calendar event built client-side from the same data (the earliest pick on the board, or the no-play all-day event with the closest side), two channel cards with "Add to calendar" (`webcal://` link, rewritten from `location` when served over http(s), else the `data-site` attribute), "Open the feed", and copy-link buttons; every page's `<head>` has the `rel="alternate"` RSS link and the footer has a Subscribe link; the Free plan's button points at the section; the digest has a one-line pointer under its tiles. The sample badge in the section follows `data-sample-flag="picks"`.
- Base URL: `DEFAULT_BASE` in `scripts/feeds.js` and the `data-site` / `webcal` attributes in `index.html` carry the GitHub Pages URL. Change all three when the domain changes. Calendar clients refetch on their own schedule (Apple and Outlook roughly hourly, Google up to a day), so the feed is the faster channel.

## Second price source: SportsGameOdds (`scripts/sgo.js`)

Free "amateur" tier: 2,500 event objects a month (every event in a response costs one, usage endpoint is free), 10 requests a minute (the adapter spaces calls 6.2 s apart), leagues NBA, NFL, MLB, NHL, NCAAB, NCAAF, MLS, UCL (no EPL: the Premier League stays Odds-API-only; MLS is wired since 2026-11-05, on the `reg` period, 90 minutes and stoppage time: the three-way moneyline `points-home-reg-ml3way-home` / `points-away-reg-ml3way-away` / `points-all-reg-ml3way-draw`, de-vigged across all three, and the total `points-all-reg-ou-over` / `-under`; the total's ids are on the vendor's MLS page, the three-way ones follow its oddID pattern and are unconfirmed until a live response shows them, and a wrong id only means no odds for that pick), nine books (DraftKings, FanDuel, BetMGM, Caesars, ESPN BET, Bovada, PointsBet, Unibet; `williamhill` is folded into Caesars) and the vendor's own de-vigged `fairOdds` with `openFairOdds`/`openFairSpread`/`openFairOverUnder`. **No Pinnacle**, so it never finds edges; The Odds API keeps discovery and the anchor. What it does:

- **Board refresh** (`sgo.refresh`, in both the full run and `--track`): games already on the board are fetched by `eventIDs` in one call per league (the id is remembered as `sgoEventId`); a new game is found with a 30-minute `startsAfter/startsBefore` window around its start (which also returns the other games tipping then, each costing an entity). For the pick's side it records `sgo` {books on our exact line (title → price), n, best, consensus fair, vendor fair, open, now} and appends `sgoHistory` when anything changes. Those prices are merged into `prices` (fresher for shared books, new books added; the logged `odds`/`book` never change), so line shopping and parlays see nine books. The breakdown gets `mkt` ("The market (N books) opened X, now Y") as support or risk by direction, and a "better price available" line when one of its books beats the logged price.
- **Close** (`sgo.close` at grading): the game's record after it ends still carries every book's last price and the vendor fair, plus `results.game` scores. Preferred over ESPN's DraftKings close.
- **Budget**: `RULES.sgoReserve` (150) entities are never touched; `sgoOk()` checks `/account/usage` once per run. Rough spend: board games × (2 full runs + up to 8 hourly track runs inside the 8-hour pre-game window) plus one per graded pick, about 1,500–2,000 a month on a busy board. Without `SGO_API_KEY` every call is skipped and nothing else changes.
- **Player props** (`PROP_STATS`, `playerIndex`, `findPlayer`, `propSnapshot`, `playerProps`, `nightEvents`, `eventFor`): every player's lines ride along in the game's event object at no extra entity cost when the `oddIDs` filter is off (`events({ allOdds: true })`). `update-props.js` uses this: one call with a start window around tonight's games (one entity per game, about 14 an NBA night) and, per player, the main line per stat (the number the most books quote on both sides, ties to the vendor's `bookOverUnder`; then `bookOverUnder`, `fairOverUnder` and the most common one-sided number as fallbacks), every book's price on that exact line for over and under, the best book per side, and the fair probability per side: the vendor's de-vigged pair when `fairOverUnder` is on that line, else our own de-vig across the books quoting both sides. Players are matched by normalised name (accents, suffixes and hyphens stripped) from the event's `players` map, or read back out of the odd ids (`LUKA_DONCIC_1_NBA`) when the map is missing; last name plus first initial is the fallback when unique.

## Props research and lines

`scripts/update-props.js` runs in the same workflow step sequence as the picks (both daily runs) and never fails the job. It also writes `data/faces.js` (see Team identity); `scripts/update-teams.js` runs right after it. It uses ESPN's public, undocumented endpoints with no key: scoreboard (tonight's games), every team's schedule (points allowed, ranked 1 = allows most), rosters of the teams playing (headshot, position, injury status), and each player's game log. Rotation players only (14+ minutes a game), up to 9 per team, last 10 regular-season games kept. About 300-500 ESPN requests per run with a 90 ms delay.

**Lines (shipped 2026-11-04 as the session's bold change; the owner asked to "put the props page together").** After the ESPN pass, `attachLines()` makes one SportsGameOdds call for the night (see Second price source: player props) and writes `p.lines` per player and `data.lines` as the summary. Budget: it skips when `SGO_API_KEY` is missing, when `--no-lines` is passed, or when the month's remaining entities minus tonight's games would dip under `RULES.sgoReserve` (150, the same backstop as the board). The workflow passes `--no-lines` on the morning run and spends on the afternoon run and manual runs (`props_only` input: props, lines and team identity only, no Odds API credits), so lines cost about 14 entities a night, roughly 420 a month on top of the board's spend. Lines are posted by most books in the afternoon; the morning run would mostly find nothing.

**The grade (owner decision, 2026-11-04, the same evening): props and plays are graded on game data, never against other books' prices, and the page must say so.** The posted line is only the number to clear; the books' fair and prices take no part in the probability, the side or the rank. Client-side in `props.html`, `sideFor(p, k, line)` grades one stat at one number: `weightedHit()` is the player's hit rate against the number over the last ten games with the last five weighted 1.0 and the next five 0.6, shrunk toward even by three phantom hits and three misses (`PHANTOM`; 3-for-3 reads 60%, not 100%); `project()` is a projection (0.5 × L5 + 0.3 × L10 + 0.2 × season) scaled by the square root of the minutes trend (L5 ÷ season, clamped 0.7–1.3), by the opponent's points allowed relative to the league average (exponent 0.6 for points and PRA, 0.4 for threes, 0.25 for rebounds and assists; `LEAGUE_PA` is the mean across tonight's opponents), −4% on a back-to-back, +2% on 3–10 days' rest, blended 15% toward the home/road split when there are 4+ games, and −5% with a wider spread on any injury listing; the probability of the over is `1 − Φ((line − proj) / sd)` with sd the player's last-ten standard deviation floored per stat (`SD_FLOOR`) and widened by a quarter. Our probability of the over is the mean of the two; the favoured side is whichever is above even; the grade is High ≥ 70%, Medium ≥ 62%, Lean ≥ 56%, else "Coin flip" (calibrated on the first live night: 809 two-sided lines, median 57%, about 6% High, 24% Medium, 43% coin flips) (`GRADES`, reusing the confidence badge classes plus `.badge-flip`). Each side carries its reasons: the hit count against the number, the projection with its inputs, and whichever of minutes, matchup, rest, split, injury and the stat's own trend apply, split into for / "But:" against / neutral. `bestSide(p)` is the player's top side across the stats that are **boardable**: a line the books quote on both sides (`twoSided`: at least one book price over and under on that exact number; the vendor's consensus alone, or a one-sided alternate ladder, is not a line) and at least `MIN_GAMES` (4) logged games; a card still grades a thinner sample or a typed number and lists "Small sample" among the reasons. The **props board** lists every best side in the filtered list sorted `better()`: our probability, then raw L10 hit rate, then sample size; no tier or divider; the row shows the side, line and stat, the projection, the best price only as "where to bet it", our probability with the bar (tick = raw hit rate), the hit pill, the grade badge and the for/against counts; ten rows then "Show all"; "Open card" scrolls to the card, switches it to that stat and focuses the line box. The rank carries onto the card as a chip beside the name. Each card opens on its ranked stat with the posted line typed in (stat chips show the line), two side pills (our probability per side, best price and book, the favoured side marked) with the grade badge, the hit pill on the favoured side, and a "Why over/under" list under the chart; typing any other number regrades everything from the game data for that number, with a "Market line" reset. Overrides are stored as `{ v, m }` (value and the posted line it was typed over) so a stale override gives way to a fresh line. Sort defaults to "Likeliest first" (the board's order; unpriced players after it by heat), with "Form" (hit rate), hottest, season average, minutes and "Most reasons" as opt-ins; without any lines the board is hidden, the two sorts are disabled, "Hottest" is the default and every card still grades a typed line the same way. The how-to under the board, the rule line under the title, the board summary and the notice all state that the grade is game data only and not other books' prices. **Plays follow the same rule** since the same evening: the game model in `scripts/model.js` (see Data pipeline, step 3).

Signals (`signals()` in the script) are confluences, not bets: a stat up or down 12%+ over the last 5 vs the season, minutes up or down 10%+, a home/road scoring split of 12%+, back-to-back or 3+ days' rest, opponent in the top 8 or bottom 8 for points allowed, a steady scorer (last-10 standard deviation under 22% of the average), and any injury listing. The page lets the visitor type a line per stat and shows over hit rates for L5, L10 and the season; typed lines are kept in localStorage.

## Line shopping, live status, digest

- **Line shopping** is client-side from `prices`. `EdgeLine.lineShop(pick)` sorts every book best to worst and counts how many still leave our probability an edge of at least the pick's own bar (`EdgeLine.barOf(p)`: `p.bar` when the pick carries one, else the file's floor, 3 points, for picks posted before 2026-11-05; "clear the bar", the Lean threshold, `MARKET_DATA.threshold`); `sole` is true when exactly one book does. Card fronts show the top three prices as chips plus "+N more" and a "One book only" badge; the breakdown dialog shows the full ladder with implied, edge and a state per book, and a warning when the edge lives at one book; the table view has a Books column (`clears/total`). Book abbreviations live in `BOOK_ABBR` in `js/app.js`.
- **Live status** (`js/live.js`) runs only when `PICKS_DATA.sample` is false and the page has `[data-live]` slots. For each pick within its window (20 minutes before `commence` to 5 hours after; no `commence` = today's scoreboard) it fetches ESPN's scoreboard for that sport and ET date (one request per sport+date per tick, cached 20 s), finds the game by `context.espnEventId` or team names, and renders a pill: `LIVE · clock · AWAY a–h HOME · covering by 3.5 / needs 7 more / leading by 4 / over by 2 / on the number`, or `Final · won (unofficial)`. Polls every 60 s while a game is in progress, every 5 min otherwise, never while the tab is hidden. It never grades anything; the pipeline does. ESPN's scoreboard returns `access-control-allow-origin: *`, so no proxy is needed. Failures are silent.
- **Digest** (`digest.html`) reads the same two data files. The slate is one list in the site order (game day first, then positive edge, then likeliest) with a `.day-divider` heading where the day changes (`dayBreaks`, also written into the copied text); each row's start label ("Today · 7:00 PM ET" / "Tomorrow" / weekday) comes from `commence` and falls back to parsing the `start` label; a bare weekday in a label always means a later day. `@media print` in `css/styles.css` switches the tokens to a light palette and hides the backdrop, header, buttons and slip, so "Print or save as PDF" gives a clean one-pager. "Copy as text" writes a plain-text version (`EdgeLineDigest.asText()`) to the clipboard.
